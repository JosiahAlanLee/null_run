# THE PLEAT — the lagging that holds

**Status: PROPOSAL throughout.** Sheet 10. I have not seen the image; §1 is Claude's description, quoted.
Origin proposed **never human**: a body grown in pipe lagging and inspection plate, in the cistern's plant
rooms.

## 1. What the description establishes

| Description detail | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Hunched, crab-like body of overlapping dark slate-grey rib plates | The plates are the outside. They overlap like a folded fan or a bellows, not like armour strapped on. |
| Yellow fibrous insulation bursting from the seams | The body is packed with lagging. The fibre is the soft inside; the plates are the container. |
| Curved claw-hook forelimbs | Two hooks, for gripping and for holding a weight, not for fine work. |
| Beak-like head | Small, low, between the shoulders; a hinge, not a face. |
| READY: gripping a concrete block | The block is not a weapon. It is a **weight**, held against something. |
| ACTION: plates snap open into a full radial fan of ribs and yellow membrane, as it slams down | The whole animal unfolds into a disc and drives itself into the floor. The fan is the attack and the display at once. |

The description gives three facts: **it is folded**, **it is full of fibre**, and **it is holding a block**.
The first two are anatomy. The third is the encounter.

## 3. Lore proposals

### 3.1 Origin (never human — proposal)

It grew in the lagging around a valve that was supposed to be drained and was not. The lagging learned the
one thing lagging is for: **to keep a difference between two sides**. The plates are the inspection hatches;
the fibre is the insulation; the hooks are the tools that opened the hatches. It has no memory of the
valve, only the habit of holding.

The block is the second fact. It found a concrete block — a counterweight, a lid, a piece of the works — and
put it on the fitting. The fitting stopped leaking. The Pleat has held the block there ever since. It is
not a pet; it is a **maintenance decision made by a body that has never been told the job is finished**.

### 3.2 What it wants (proposal)

To keep the pressure on the correct side. It does not want to hurt anyone; it wants the hatch shut, and
people are on the wrong side of the hatch. The fan is not a threat display in its own understanding: it is
how the body redistributes load. It is a gasket that has learned to brace.

The office's word for the species is **lagging**. The thermal survey calls the yellow fibre "serviceable".
The survey has never been inside the fan.

### 3.3 Strata and room tags (proposal)

**Primary: stratum 3, flooded cistern.** Tags: `pump room`, `valve chamber`, `settling-tank rim`,
`pipe gallery`, `hatch`, `sump`. It is always at a fitting, never in open water.

**Secondary: stratum 0, plant rooms.** Tags: `boiler annex`, `service corridor`, `riser`, `inspection
hatch`. The transit's plant is newer and the lagging there is thinner; it is smaller there.

**Absent from:** the ossuary (dry stone, no pressure), the scriptorium (bronze and paper, nothing to hold),
the root cathedral (the growth there does not use fittings). The cistern is its centre; the player learns
that every hatch in the cistern may have a Pleat on the other side.

### 3.4 Relationships

- **Underprint** — both are layered bodies, and they must never blur. The Underprint is **plaster**: it
  remembers being leaned on and wants contact. The Pleat is **lagging**: it remembers pressure and wants
  distance. One leans toward you; the other braces away.
- **Ballast Husk** — both plant-room equipment. The Husk was operated and has a maw; the Pleat operates
  itself and has no mouth. A Husk's arc can weld a Pleat's seams shut; the Pleat then cannot open, and
  will die slowly. This is a possible authored interaction, not a simulated one.
- **Circuit** — the Circuit routes through warm lagging. A Pleat's seam is a junction the Circuit uses
  without asking. A Pleat opening on a Circuit tether severs it; the Pleat does not notice.
- **White Bloom** — both cistern. The Bloom wants water; the Pleat wants pressure. They share a room and
  ignore each other. The Bloom's spikes do not grow through the Pleat's block.
- **Hem** — she will not go into the cistern, and the Pleat is one of the reasons. She calls it "the
  lagging" and does not consider it an animal.

## 4. Encounter design

### 4.1 Stat block (starting values)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| HP | **200**. Closed plates **0.4×** (sacrificial); open yellow membrane **1.5×**; beak 1.25×; the block is a **shield** (0 damage, blocks the front 120°) while gripped. |
| Size | 1.3 m tall folded; 4.4 m across the open fan; 1.4 m hook reach; block 0.5 m cube |
| Speed | scuttle 140 u/s; fold 90; slam is stationary; it does not run |
| Attacks | fan slam 22 within **2.2 m** (the whole open fan; 40-tick wind + 15-tick slam); fibre puff 4 + 3 s accuracy penalty in a 2 m cone when the seams first open; claw hook 14 (1.4 m) |
| Fairness caps | one slam per 5 s; the fan opens before every slam (1.2 s); **beyond 2.2 m is always safe**; the block is always visible; no slam while the block is being swapped |

### 4.2 Behaviour

| State | Ticks | What it does | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FOLDED (idle loop) | 4 × 8 = 32 | Crouched over the block, plates shut in overlapping folds, yellow fibre only at the seams; a slow bellows breathing. | It is holding. The block is the tell. |
| GRIP | 3 × 7 = 21 | Both hooks clamp the block; the body settles; the seam temperature rises. | The block is part of the encounter, not scenery. |
| NOTICE | 3 × 6 = 18 | The beak lifts; the seams part a finger's width; a puff of fibre; it rotates to put the block between you and its body. | It is deciding which side you are on. |
| OPEN (wind) | 4 × 10 = 40 | The plates unfold from the bottom up; the yellow membrane shows; the bellows inhales; the block is set down. | **The telegraph.** The fan is opening. |
| SLAM | 3 × 5 = 15 | The fan reaches full radial spread and drives down. Dust, a pressure thump, a ring of fibre. | The attack. Beyond 2.2 m is safe; the disc is not. |
| FOLD (recover) | 4 × 9 = 36 | It folds seam by seam, slowly, and re-grips the block. The membrane is exposed for the first 24 ticks. | The damage window. Shoot the yellow. |
| SKITTER | 4 × 6 = 24 | A low crab walk, block held in one hook, the other hook testing the floor. | It repositions to a fitting. It does not chase far. |
| SWAP | 4 × 8 = 32 | It sets the old block down, tests a new one with both hooks, and accepts or refuses. | The puzzle state. It will not fight during a swap. |
| HURT | 2 × 5 = 10 | A plate cracks; fibre puffs; it braces harder. | It does not flinch away; it flinches **down**. |
| DEATH | 4 × 8 = 32 | The fan opens once, all the way, and does not close; the fibre settles; the block rolls free. | The fitting releases. The room gets wet. |
| CORPSE | 1 | A collapsed fan of slate plates, yellow fibre, one hook still hooked around nothing. | The block is now an item. |

The sheet's named poses map as: `ready` = FOLDED/GRIP, `action` = OPEN → SLAM. The open fan is both the
telegraph and the hit; the fold is the recovery.

### 4.3 The telegraph

**The seams open bottom-first, and the fibre puffs.** The plates unfold from the floor upward, the yellow
membrane shows between them, the bellows inhales audibly, and the beak clicks twice. The full fan is a
4.4 m disc: everything within 2.2 m is under the slam. A player inside that radius must leave; the fan is
slow, and a walk clears it. The counter-intuitive part is the **fold**, not the open: the open fan looks
like the dangerous state, and it is also the damage window (the yellow membrane is 1.5×); the closed
plates look vulnerable and are nearly immune (0.4×). The first slam should be taught in a room with
nothing else in it.

### 4.4 Counterplay

- **Leave the fan.** Beyond 2.2 m the slam cannot reach; the fan is slow and a walk clears it. Do not
try to stand under it; the body occupies the centre.
- **Shoot the yellow when open.** Membrane 1.5×; the fold is 36 ticks with the membrane exposed for 24.
- **Flank it when closed.** The block shields 120° of front. Plates are 0.4×, so shooting them is a waste;
  shoot the exposed seams at the sides, or wait.
- **Do not stand in the fibre puff.** It is not damage; it is a 3 s accuracy penalty and a cough. It is
  also the room telling you the seams are open.
- **The block is not a weapon.** It never throws it. If it loses the block, it becomes slower and more
  aggressive until it finds another (see 4.5).

### 4.5 Non-lethal / puzzle route — THE COUNTERWEIGHT

The block is the encounter's key, and the key is a **maintenance problem**.

- **The fitting.** The Pleat is holding a hatch, a floor plate or a pipe collar shut. The hatch is the
  route to the next room (or a shortcut back). The Pleat will not let go while it is holding.
- **Offer a heavier block.** A concrete block from the environment (a fallen lintel, a stack of pavers, a
  piece of the basin's pumice) can be presented at the SWAP state. It tests the weight with both hooks. If
  the new block is **heavier**, it accepts, sets the old one down, and re-grips the fitting. The hatch is
  free; the Pleat stays. This is the clean solution and the one the documents hint at ("the lagging has
  been offered a new block twice and refused both" — both were equal weight).
- **Wedge the hatch.** A steel wedge or a pipe section jammed into the fitting lets the Pleat release
  without a replacement. The Pleat is confused, then relieved, then follows the player at a distance,
  looking for a new fitting. It is not hostile in this state; it is **unemployed**.
- **Steal the block.** Possible, and a bad idea. The Pleat follows, the hatch vents (steam hazard in the
  room below), and the player has a 0.5 m cube to carry. It can be used as a step, a weight or a thrown
  object. The Pleat will take it back if it can.
- **The lift.** In the cistern's pump room the block is the missing counterweight for a small goods lift.
  Putting it on the lift makes the lift work; the Pleat, if alive, rides it. This is a later set-piece,
  not the first encounter.

The fight exists for players who shoot first. It is winnable and expensive: 200 HP at 0.4× on the plates
means the fight is a waiting game for the fold, and the fold only comes after a slam.

### 4.6 What it does not do (deliberate limits)

No wall-climbing, no ceiling-walking, no splitting into two Pleats, no pretending to be a pipe (it is not
a Mimic), no ranged attack beyond the fibre puff, no grabbing the player (the hooks hold fittings, not
people), no pressure-wave knockback beyond 0.5 m. It is a heavy, slow, local animal. It never leaves its
fitting voluntarily.

## 5. Sound cues (in words)

- **Idle:** a bellows breathing, slow and wet; the tick of cooling metal; fibre settling in the seams like
  sand in a bag.
- **Notice:** a beak click, twice, like a stapler; a seam parting with a dry book-opening crack.
- **Open:** a long inhale through cloth; the plates unfolding like a fan of heavy card; a rising pressure
  whine from the fitting.
- **Slam:** a single low thump with no echo, the sound a mattress makes when it hits a floor; then dust.
- **Fold:** the same fan, closing, slower; a soft clatter of plates; a wheeze.
- **Swap:** concrete on concrete, one clean knock; a hook testing the weight; a pause; a second knock.
- **Death:** the fan opens all the way and stops; the fibre sighs out; water starts moving somewhere below.
- **The fitting released:** a pipe ticking as it cools, and a drain swallowing.
