# THE WHITE BLOOM — the filter that stands up

**Status: PROPOSAL throughout.** Sheet 14. I have not seen the image; §1 is Claude's description, quoted.
Origin proposed **never human**: a basin bloom of porous white growth, rust-red sinew and bone-white limbs.
This is the batch's area-denial creature; the wet-floor rule in §4.3 is the condition of approval.

## 1. What the description establishes

| Description detail | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Tall, spindly, many-legged bone-white limbs with rust-red sinew | A tall, thin frame: legs like a heron's, sinew like rust. It is not armoured; it is **strung**. |
| Upper body erupts into porous, coral- or fungus-like white growths | The bloom is the body's top, not a hat. It is porous, soft and alive; it is the filter. |
| Feet are spongy, porous mounds on a wet floor | The feet are the water intake. They leave wet rings. They are the creature's dependence. |
| Bony claw-mandibles | The only hard weapon on it: a beak of bone, for filtering and for snapping. |
| ACTION: white crystalline or fungal spikes erupt from the wet floor around it | The floor is the weapon. The Bloom does not throw anything; it **grows** the attack out of the water. |

The description gives three facts: **it stands on sponges**, **its top is a filter**, and **its attack
comes out of the floor**. The encounter is about the floor.

## 3. Lore proposals

### 3.1 Origin (never human — proposal)

It grew in the settling basin's filter bed, where the water is slow and the sludge is deep. The bloom is
the filter's soft top; the legs are the filter's stand; the feet are the filter's intake. It learned to
stand because standing keeps the bloom above the water and the feet in it. It is not a predator. It is a
**water treatment process that has become an animal**, and it is still treating the water.

This is the batch's one genuinely sessile organism, and the one that most deserves the word "growth". It
has no face, no eyes, no mouth in the human sense; the mandibles are a filter grate that can close. It
does not know the player is a person. It knows the player is a **disturbance in the water**.

### 3.2 What it wants (proposal)

Still water. It stands where the water is slowest, and it grows spikes when the water is disturbed. The
spikes are not a defence in the sense of a threat display; they are **the filter clearing a blockage**.
The player is a blockage. The bloom is not angry; it is doing its job, which is the most frightening
thing about it.

The office's word for the species is **bloom**. The water board has a bloom-control procedure. The
procedure is "none". The procedure has worked for eleven years.

### 3.3 Strata and room tags (proposal)

**Primary: stratum 3, flooded cistern / settling basin.** Tags: `sludge flat`, `filter bed`, `settling
tank`, `wet margin`, `screen`, `sump`, `grit channel`. It is always where the floor is wet.

**Secondary: stratum 4, root cathedral (damp bays).** Tags: `damp bay`, `seep`, `standing pool`, `rubble
slope`. The cathedral's damp is enough for a small Bloom; the dry nave is not.

**Absent from:** the transit (dry, fluorescent, no standing water), the ossuary (dry stone; the Bloom's
feet would dry out in minutes), the scriptorium (dry, bronze, no water). The dry strata are safe from it,
and the player learns that from the wet rings: a dry floor is a wall to the Bloom.

### 3.4 Relationships

- **Vore Spindle** — the other basin organism. The Spindle is **vermin**: it launches one homing spore and
  is fought at range. The Bloom is **growth**: it denies the floor and is fought by not standing in water.
  Never give the Bloom a projectile and never give the Spindle a floor eruption.
- **Ravel** — the other filter. The Ravel keeps the grit; the Bloom keeps the water. They share a room and
  ignore each other. The Ravel's shards do not pierce the Bloom's growths; the Bloom's spikes do not grow
  through the Ravel's crest. They are two answers to the same job.
- **Unaddressed** — both pale, porous and growth-like, and they must not blur. The Unaddressed's pale
  baffles are **plaster ears** (they notice); the Bloom's growths are **water filters** (they clean). The
  Unaddressed is in the cathedral; the Bloom is in the basin. If a Bloom appears in a damp cathedral bay,
  keep it small and keep the Unaddressed's plaster out of the same room.
- **Circuit** — water is a hard wall to the Circuit. The Bloom lives in the wall. A Circuit trio will not
  cross a Bloom's wet ring; a Bloom will not grow through a live cable. The two are each other's boundary.
- **Pleat** — both cistern. The Pleat holds pressure; the Bloom holds water. The Pleat's block is dry; the
  Bloom's feet are wet; they share a pump room and stay on their own sides.
- **Span** — the Span will not span wet ground; the Bloom makes wet ground. In the basin the Span is
  absent and the Bloom is not.

The sheet's named poses map as: `ready` = FILTER, `action` = BLOOM. DRAW and SETTLE are the two states the
encounter is built on.

### 4.3 The telegraph and the wet-floor contract

**The wet ring spreads, then the tips show.** Before every bloom: the floor within 3 m goes wet-shiny, small
bubbles rise, white tips appear through the water, the mandibles open, and a kettle hiss sounds. The
telegraph is **1.1 s**. The spikes erupt in a **ring at 1.0–3.0 m**; the centre (under the body) is safe but
occupied, and the dry floor beyond 3 m is the usable safe zone. The ring is the attack.

The rule that makes it fair and readable:

- **Spikes need wet floor.** The bloom can only erupt where the floor is wet. Dry floor, a raised grate, a
  step, a rubble mound or a dry ledge is a hard safe zone. The level must show the wet/dry boundary
  clearly: a visible waterline, a wet sheen, a dry dust edge. Do not rely on a subtle material change.
- **The wet ring is the tell.** The Bloom's feet spread a wet ring even at rest. The player can read the
  radius before the fight starts. A Bloom on a dry floor is a WILT state and cannot bloom at all.
- **The spikes are level geometry, not sprites.** They need a spawn marker per spike, a collision volume,
  a 4 s lifetime, a crumble animation and a white grit decal. They must not spawn inside the player; if
  the player is in the ring, the spike under them is skipped (the ring has gaps where the player stands,
  or the player is pushed to the nearest gap). The impale is a 2 s movement lock, not a damage-over-time.
- **The ring has 8–12 spikes, not a solid wall.** There are gaps. A player who runs through the ring at
  the right moment can cross; a player who stands still is caught. The gaps are fixed per eruption and
  visible in the tips.
- **No bloom while the player is impaled.** The 7 s cooldown starts after SETTLE, not after BLOOM.

### 4.4 Counterplay

- **Fight from dry ground.** The single most important counter. A dry ledge, a grate, a rubble mound, a
  step: the Bloom cannot reach it. The room should always offer one.
- **Drain the floor.** A sluice valve, a pump switch, a cracked tank: draining the room puts the Bloom in
  WILT and ends the fight without damage. This is the puzzle route (4.5).
- **Break the feet.** 1.25× and they are the water intake; a Bloom with two broken feet cannot draw enough
  water to bloom. It retreats.
- **The mandibles are the weak point when open.** 1.5×; the DRAW state opens them wide. A prepared player
  can end the fight in the telegraph.
- **Do not stand in the wet ring.** The ring is visible before the fight; the telegraph is 1.1 s; the
  player has time to leave.
- **Do not chase it into the water.** It is slow on land and slower in water; the player is slower in
  water too. Fight it on the dry side.

### 4.5 Non-lethal / puzzle route — DO NOT DISTURB THE WATER

The Bloom is the batch's cleanest non-lethal encounter: it does not want anything from the player, and it
ignores anything that does not disturb the water.

1. **Walk around the wet ring.** The Bloom filters; it does not block. A player who walks the dry edge and
   does not splash passes without a fight. This is the default and the room should teach it.
2. **Drain the room.** A sluice valve or a pump switch lowers the water; the Bloom WILTs, folds, and
   retreats to the last puddle. The room is now dry and safe; the Bloom is alive in the corner. This is
   the "solve the room" route and the one the water board's log hints at.
3. **Use the bloom as a barrier.** Disturb the water on the far side (throw a stone, stamp, strike a pipe)
   and the Bloom erupts there. The spikes last 4 s and block movement. A player with a pursuer can put a
   Bloom between them and the pursuer. The Bloom does not care; it is clearing a blockage.
4. **Trigger it at a distance.** The Bloom responds to vibration, not to the player specifically. A thrown
   object, a shot into the water, a dropped pipe: the bloom erupts where the disturbance is. This is the
   decoy mechanic, and it is deliberately unlike the Circuit's lamp decoy: a sound in the water vs a dead
   circuit.
5. **Do not harvest the bloom.** The growths are not an item; the white grit is. A player who tries to cut
   the bloom gets a face full of spores that are not a mechanic and should not become one (see 4.6).

The fight is optional. The room should have a dry path, a drain, or a decoy. If it has none, the Bloom is
an unfair tax on exploration.

### 4.6 What it does not do (deliberate limits)

No projectile, no spore cloud, no infection, no poison damage-over-time, no climbing, no chasing beyond a
walk, no blooming on dry floor, no spikes through the player's feet without a gap/telegraph, no healing,
no splitting, no boss scaling. It is a sessile filter with a floor attack. The horror is that it is
**indifferent**, not that it is fast.

## 5. Sound cues (in words)

- **Idle:** water dripping through sponges; a slow wet pulse; the soft crackle of the bloom's pores; the
  mandibles clicking like a filter grate.
- **Notice:** the dripping stops; a single deep bubble; the bloom's pores opening with a sound like a
  sponge squeezed underwater.
- **Draw:** a kettle hiss rising; water sucking into the feet; a low, wet rumble under the floor.
- **Bloom:** a hard, wet crack; a dozen white tips punching through water; grit falling; the bloom flaring
  with a papery rush.
- **Settle:** the spikes crumbling, one after another, like sugar; the pores closing; a long exhale.
- **Snap:** bone on bone, a clean click; no growl.
- **Death:** the bloom collapsing inward; water spreading; the legs folding; one last bubble.
- **Wilt:** a dry, papery shrink; the feet hardening; the wet ring receding.
