# THE REUNION — two people, one application

**Status: PROPOSAL throughout.** Sheet 12. I have not seen the image; §1 is Claude's description, quoted.
Origin proposed **formerly human, two persons**, and the relationship between them is deliberately left
open. This is the batch's dignity-critical sheet; read §3.1 and `NOTES.md` item 2 before using it.

## 1. What the description establishes

| Description detail | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| A woman (mustard shirt, suspenders) and a man (green cardigan) | Two adults, dressed as themselves. The clothes are ordinary and specific; they are not costumes. |
| Joined at the hips into a single four-legged body | One body, two people. Four legs, two torsos, two heads. The join is at the hip, not the spine. |
| A stitched, patched trouser-and-leather harness | The join is **made**: a garment, repaired, numbered, institutional. It is not a growth. |
| They face opposite ways | The defining fact. They cannot look at the same thing without turning the whole body. |
| READY: both braced, wary, looking different directions | The default is a disagreement held in balance. |
| ACTION: both lunge outward in opposite directions, arms outstretched, as if pulling apart | The attack is a **wishbone**: the body pulls against itself, and everything between the two arms is caught. |

The description gives three facts: **the join is a garment**, **they face opposite ways**, and **their
attack is a disagreement made physical**. The encounter is about the disagreement, not the join.

## 3. Lore proposals

### 3.1 Origin (formerly human, two persons — proposal, relationship open)

They are two people who asked to be together. The office granted the request in the way offices grant
things: it issued a garment and a case number. The harness is not a punishment and not a experiment. It is
**paperwork with straps**. They do not remember the procedure as an operation; they remember it as a
form, a wait, and a fitting. They are still themselves. That is the point.

Their relationship is **not specified** and should never be specified in-game. They could be siblings,
spouses, parent and adult child, colleagues, or two strangers who applied for the same reason. The
application's relationship field is redacted. This is not coyness; it is the dignity rule. The moment the
game says "husband and wife", it invites the audience to read the join as a joke about marriage. The
moment it says "brother and sister", it invites a different joke. Keep it a redaction, and let the two
people be two people.

What is fixed: they have known each other long enough to be rude to each other, and they are fond of each
other in the way of long acquaintance. They are not telepathic. They do not share pain. They do not finish
each other's sentences. They interrupt each other. They have separate opinions about everything except the
case number.

### 3.2 What they want (proposal)

To go in the right direction. Each is certain of the direction; the certainties are opposite. One
wants **down** — an appointment, a name to find, a room they remember. The other wants **up** — enough, or
something left behind, or hunger. Which is which should vary by instance; the sheet does not assign it, and
neither does this file. Neither will say why to a stranger. Both will ask the stranger to decide, because a
decision from outside is the only kind they can accept without one of them losing.

The office's word for them is **the joint**, which is also the word for the harness, the case and the room.

This is the encounter: **they are not hostile; they are stuck.** The player is the tie-breaker. The fight
happens if the player tries to force the body, cut the harness, or walk through the join without a word.

### 3.3 Strata and room tags (proposal)

**Primary: stratum 0, institutional transit.** Tags: `arrivals hall`, `reunification desk`, `waiting
room`, `bench facing the stair`, `lost property`, `platform 3`. They are where the office puts people who
have just been joined.

**Secondary: stratum 1, limestone ossuary.** Tags: `chapel`, `bench facing the stair`, `north gallery`.
They came down for the appointment and cannot agree to go further.

**Absent from:** the cistern (the harness would wet and rot; both refuse, jointly, for once), the
scriptorium (the desks face the walls; there is no room to turn), the root cathedral (no desk, no
direction, and the stair does not go there). The cistern is the one place they will not follow, and the
one place the player can be sure of a clean exit.

### 3.4 Relationships

- **Hem** — she knew one of them. She will not say which, and she will not mend the harness. She says it is
  not a garment that wants mending. She is right.
- **Calmor** — wants to measure them separately and cannot. It has tried to fit them with a single
  measurement and filed the result under "incomplete". It considers the harness a restraint and would like
  to adjust it. They will not let it near the straps.
- **Applicant** — photographed them separately, before. The two photographs show two people standing in
  the same spot at different times. The Applicant now points at the gap between the photographs. It is
  the only creature that understands the join as a composition.
- **Repetiel** — delivered the reunification notice. Both signed. Repetiel remembers two signatures and
  one envelope, and considers the case closed.
- **Span** — they will not cross it. They cannot agree on the order, and the Span will not take two
  persons who are one body. (Load rating: two persons or one cart. They are neither.)
- **Visitor** — he knew them before. He does not remember from where. They do not remind him.

### 4.1 Stat block (starting values)

The sheet's named poses map as: `ready` = BRACED, `action` = WISHBONE. DISAGREE, TALK and PASS are the
states that make the non-lethal route possible.

### 4.3 The telegraph

**Both look past you in opposite directions and set their feet.** The harness stitches pop one by one
(visible and audible), and they take a single shared breath — the only synchronised thing they do. The
wishbone is a **line**, not a circle: the danger is the axis between their outstretched arms. Step
perpendicular, or close to the join, which is their blind spot. The telegraph is 0.7 s; the lunge is fast
but the line is fixed before it starts.

### 4.4 Counterplay

- **Stand perpendicular.** The wishbone sweeps the axis; the sides are safe. This is the whole fight.
- **Use a pillar or doorframe.** They cannot pass on opposite sides of a column; the harness catches. A
  narrow door forces them to turn together, which takes 3 s and is the best punish window.
- **Stairs.** They are slow and must go sideways. Do not make this a joke; make it the body's limit.
- **Do not attack the harness.** It is 1.5× damage and it kills both. The game should not offer a "cut
  them free" prompt. If the player shoots it anyway, the harness holds (it is stitched to hold), and both
  subjects turn on the player with a single purpose for 10 s. That is the only time they agree on a direction.
- **The join is a blind spot, not a weak point.** Attacks from the join do 0.5× and provoke the backhand.
  It is a place to stand, not a place to hit.
- **Talk.** The encounter has a full non-combat route (4.5). The fight is what happens when the player
  treats two people as one obstacle.

### 4.5 Non-lethal route — SETTLE THE DIRECTION

They need a decision from outside, and the player can give one. The route has four shapes, any of which
ends the encounter without a fight:

1. **Open the door they are facing.** One wants through the door; the other wants back the way they came.
   If the player opens the door (or unlocks it), the one who wants through takes a step, the other resists,
   and then — because the way is now open and the argument is about the way — they go together. The
   encounter ends with them walking through, still arguing. The player does not choose which one wins; the
   **door** does.
2. **Return a lost item.** A glove, a ticket, a name tag, a letter from lost property. Give it to the one
   who wants it. The other is annoyed, then interested, then agrees to go as far as the next room to hear
   the rest. They leave together. The item is consumed.
3. **Show them a notice.** A timetable, a room number, a posted change of address. The notice settles the
   direction because it is external and official. They read it together (the only time they face the same
   way), thank the player separately, and go. This is the driest and best version: the office's own paper
   is the only thing they trust.
4. **Lie to one of them.** Tell one that the appointment is up, or the other that the exit is down. The chosen
   one agrees; the other is outvoted by the body. They go. One of them is silent for the rest of the
   encounter. The record notes "direction settled by visitor; one subject silent". This is allowed and it
   is not punished mechanically; it is simply worse.

If the player does nothing, they **PASS**: they step apart as far as the harness allows and let the player
through, watching in opposite directions. They are not an obstacle unless the player makes them one. The
fight starts only if the player attacks, pushes through the join, or tries to cut the harness.

### 4.6 Dignity rules (binding)

These are not suggestions. They are the condition on which the sheet should be used.

- **Two people, not one creature.** The record names both. The death state names both. The corpse is two
  people. The camera does not frame the join as a spectacle.
- **No comedy of disagreement.** They are not a sitcom couple. The argument is quiet, repetitive and sad;
  it has been going on long enough that it has worn smooth. The player should feel they are intruding, not
  being entertained.
- **No romance implied by the join.** The relationship is redacted. Do not stage them as lovers, do not
  stage them as siblings, do not stage a wedding or a divorce. Stage two adults who are fond of each other
  and cannot agree.
- **No fetish or freak-show framing.** The harness is institutional equipment: leather, trouser fabric,
  stitching, a case tag. It is not bondage gear, not medical horror, not a circus act. It should look like
  something issued by a depot.
- **No cure.** The player cannot separate them. There is no "good ending" where they are cut apart. The
  good ending is that they walk away together, still disagreeing, which is what they asked for.
- **No mockery in the documents.** The intake record is dry, not cruel. The office's failure is that it
  granted the request literally, not that the subjects are ridiculous.

## 5. Sound cues (in words)

- **Idle:** two sets of footsteps out of phase, one forward and one back, scuffing the same floor; leather
  creaking; a stitch popping softly, now and then.
- **Notice:** both heads turning at once — a single cloth rustle, then the harness creak.
- **Disagree:** two voices, low, overlapping, not shouting; the words are not intelligible; the body rocks.
- **Wishbone wind:** stitches popping in a row, like a seam giving; a shared inhale; a leather groan.
- **Wishbone:** two arms cutting the air in opposite directions; the harness snapping taut with a low
  thrum; a single grunt from each.
- **Tangle:** the harness pulling them back; a stumble; two people apologising at different times.
- **Pass:** the harness slackening; two sets of feet stepping apart; a held breath.
- **Death:** one body down, then the other a half-beat later; the harness settling; the case tag ticking
  against the floor.
- **The notice read:** paper unfolding; two people reading at different speeds; one says "oh"; the other
  says nothing.

## 6. Sprite roles (3D artist)

This is the batch's hardest billboard. Two people facing opposite ways cannot be baked as one front-facing
sprite without one of them being a back. Two options:

- **A (recommended): two actors, one constraint.** Bake the woman and the man as separate actors with
  their own banks, joined at the hip by an engine-side harness constraint (like the Circuit's tether, but
  short and rigid). Each actor has its own facing; the constraint keeps the hip distance. This is cheaper
  per actor and solves the opposite-facing problem, at the cost of a joint constraint and a shared state
  machine. The harness itself is engine geometry (a short strap) or a small mesh.
- **B: one actor, front/back split.** Bake a single 2.2 m body with the woman facing camera-front and the
  man facing camera-back in the same sprite. This only reads at yaw 0; at other yaws the back-facing
  person's face is wrong. Not recommended.

If A: each person's pose extent is ~1.4 m; **3.0 m span at 768 px = 256 px/m; body ~1.0 m ≈ 256 px**;
pivot z = 0.95 m. The harness is engine-side.
