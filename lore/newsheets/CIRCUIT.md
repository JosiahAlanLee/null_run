# THE CIRCUIT — three bodies, one memory

**Status: PROPOSAL throughout.** Sheet: `shared/inputs/nullrun/author-sheets-20261101b/circuit.png`
("THE CIRCUIT / three bodies / shared memory"). Everything in §2 onward is mine; §1 is what the sheet shows.

## 1. What the sheet establishes (read off the sheet)

| Sheet detail | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Three bodies | One quadruped species, repeated: same anatomy, same pose language, three instances. |
| Shared memory | The sheet's own subtitle. Whatever the trio shares is memory, not a hivemind and not a hierarchy of one leader and two servants. |
| Porcelain ribs | Bowed white ribs, chipped, over a dark interior. The ribs are the outside of the animal. |
| Cable and copper musculature | Black jacketed cable bundles with exposed copper strands running between the ribs and along the limbs. Nothing fleshy. |

| Living tether | A black hose with copper strands, red band near the cut end and at the root, growing from a split root in the rear of the body. The tether-root panel shows **two** tethers leaving one body: the anatomy is hub-and-spoke, not a chain. |
| Gripping pads | Broad pads with rounded porcelain toe caps. The animal stands on them, and the side view shows it gripping rather than resting. |
| Poses | `ready` (crouched, whiskers forward) · `side` (tether arching over the back) · `linked` (three bodies, tethers taut between them) · `tether root` (rear view, two tethers from one hub) · `isolated` (one body, a severed tether lying on the floor). |

Two sheet facts drive everything below: **the hub body carries two tethers**, and **the animal has no eyes.**

## 2. Lore proposals

### 2.1 Origin (never human — proposal)

A growth in a cable tray that learned **continuity** before it learned a body. Its first achievement was
not movement but maintenance: a closed circuit that stayed closed. The three bodies are terminal equipment
that the growth assembled out of the same material it lives in — porcelain standoff insulators for ribs,
conduit for limbs, instrument cable for muscle, brass contact rods for whiskers.

This is the established cable/insulation/conduit material family, and it is the same family as the Ballast
Husk. The two are the family's opposite outcomes, and I propose the contrast explicitly: **the Husk is
equipment that was operated; the Circuit is cable that was never equipment at all.** The Husk has a maw
and a charge; the Circuit has no mouth and does not arc. It has never eaten anything.

### 2.2 What it wants (proposal)

To stay connected, and to find out whether a new thing completes a circuit, interrupts one, or is merely
in the way. It does not hunt. Its damage to the player is incidental — the whiskers carry a small current
because they are testing, not striking.

This matters for encounter design. The Circuit is not hostile on sight. It is **indifferent until the
player disturbs a live circuit** — a lamp, a junction, a gate, a cable it is using as a route. The first
contact is investigative: it touches you, decides you are neither a conductor nor a break, and loses
interest after a few seconds. The fight begins when the player damages wiring, or blocks a route the
Circuit is using, or severs a tether.

Proposal for the mundane institutional frame, which is where the tone lives: the works department calls
them **coils**. There is a maintenance schedule for them. They have been tested, logged and re-cabled.
Nobody has written down that they are animals.

### 2.3 Hub, spokes and memory (proposal)

- One body carries the split root and two tethers: the **hub**. Its two partners are **terminals**.
- While linked, the three share one record: where contact was last made, which route conducted, which
  terminal is where. This is memory in the plain sense — a log of contacts — not a mind, not a plan.
- Sever a spoke and the terminal becomes **isolated**: it keeps only its own last few contacts. It does not
  know where the others are, it stops coordinating, and it starts foraging for brass and copper (it eats
  continuity, in the only sense it can: it finds loose conductive material and holds a circuit through it).
- Kill the hub and both terminals are isolated at once. Kill both terminals and the hub is a slow,
  half-blind animal that can still trip and still lunge, but cannot triangulate.
- **Awareness stage (proposal, authored, not simulated).** A linked trio that has had a member severed
  begins to route around the place where it happened: a scrape mark in the dust shows the tether taking
  the longer way. The trio does not "learn"; the level is authored to route it differently after the
  scripted sever. This is the visible sign of the species becoming more aware of its surroundings, in the
  terms the author set: recognition, association, avoidance of one particular place.

### 2.4 Strata (proposal)

**Primary: stratum 0, institutional transit, and the vertical risers that connect stratum 0 to stratum 4.**
It travels by cable route, not by room: trays, conduits, lift shafts, the crawl space behind the
scriptorium's bronze. It is the only creature in the bestiary that routinely uses the whole building,
and the reason is material: its body is made of the thing the building uses to join floors.

**Secondary: stratum 2, bronze scriptorium.** The bronze lattice reads to it as a very large conductor.
It favours the reading floor because the metal is warm, and the overstruck plates because the overstrikes
are, to it, a repeating signal.

**Absent from:** the ossuary (dry stone, no conduit — it has nothing to hold), and the cistern (a severed
hose in standing water shorts out; it will not enter water, and water is therefore a hard boundary and a
counter — see §3.7). This absence is a feature: it gives each stratum a creature-shaped hole.

Why it fits the building's logic: the descent is a place where every layer was built to *carry* something
downward — passengers, remains, names, water, growth. The Circuit is the carrying itself, grown a body.

### 2.5 Relationship to the other species

- **Ballast Husk** — same material family, opposite trajectory. Authored ecology, not a simulated fight:
  a Husk's charge arc severs a passing tether, and a Circuit trio gives the Husk's corridor a wide berth.
  A severed tether with a scorched end means a Husk has been through.
- **Surveyor** — the line against the net. The Surveyor fixes an aim and holds it; the Circuit has no aim
  at all and locates by touch. The Surveyor's chalk lines in the cistern follow the Circuit's routes
  because both are reading the building's own wiring. Two instruments measuring the same conductor.
- **Threshold Mimic** — the Mimic roots into wall cavities; the Circuit's tethers pass through cavities.
  A tether severed cleanly at a doorway is a clue that the doorway is a mouth. Clue, not rule.
- **Unaddressed** — the important contrast, and the one to keep clean. The Unaddressed is several species
  in one place, asking what counts as a reply, with coordination that keeps failing. The Circuit is one
  organism in several bodies, with coordination that works — and it does not ask. It carries. The
  Unaddressed wants an answer; the Circuit is the wire an answer would travel on and has no interest in
  the message. (Open: whether the Unaddressed's pressure plates are wired through a Circuit trio. If they
  are, the plate sequence may have a third party in it. Do not settle.)
- **Applicant** — both commit to a fixed point and both punish recovery; they must not feel alike. The
  Applicant *looks*: a photograph, a shutter, a recorded position, a lunge at where you were. The Circuit
  *touches*: no eyes, whiskers, a tag that lasts four seconds and can be refreshed by touching you again.
  Different tells (shutter vs tether going taut), different counters (sidestep vs break the network).
- **Hem** — she calls them the mice in the wires, and tells them to keep it down. She is the only
  inhabitant who has a mundane name for them, and it is wrong in the way that matters least.

## 3. Encounter design (proposal)

**Implementation shape:** three actors plus one group controller, sharing a target and a route table. The
tether is **not** baked into any sprite: it is engine-side geometry (a tube or a short segment chain) drawn
between two actors, because a 12 m tether cannot be billboarded with the bodies. That is a real integration
cost and the single biggest question this creature raises (see `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`).

### 3.1 Stat block (starting values)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Body HP | **90** each (a linked trio = 270). Reference: imp 60, demon 150. |
| Body size | 0.95 m long, 0.55 m wide chest, 0.85–0.95 m tall at the shoulder |
| Pain | **No flinch while linked** (it braces); flinches while isolated. The absence of a hurt animation is itself a tell. |
| Speed | ready/trot 200 u/s (0.63× player run); stalking 150; contact lunge 380 for 8 ticks (≈2.2 m); isolated flee 280; isolated forage 120 |
| Ranges | whisker contact **1.2 m**, 70° arc; lunge **2.4 m**; tether span **6 m per spoke** (12 m end to end); trip span up to **9 m** |
| Damage | whisker contact **6** + 4 s tag; lunge **14** + 6 + tag; trip **10** + 0.5 s stumble (no hold); isolated bite **8** |
| Repairs | while linked, a body undamaged for 5 s recovers 20 HP, at most once per 12 s. Ribs brighten, tether pulses. Severing stops it. |
| Fairness caps | at most one lunge and one trip in any 2 s window; only one body may lunge at a time; a trip needs two bodies with pads planted |

### 3.2 States and durations (35 Hz ticks)

The sheet's named poses map onto states as follows: `ready` = READY, `linked` = STALK, `tether root` = the
rear view used for the SEVER reaction and for any single-body shot, `isolated` = ISOLATED.

| State | Ticks | Tell (must read without audio) | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| READY (loop) | 4 × 7 = 28 | Whiskers sweep a slow arc across floor and wall. Tether slack, dragging on the floor. | Read the tether: slack means unaware. Pass behind it. |
| NOTICE | 21 | Whiskers snap forward and stiffen. **All three tethers snap taut and lift off the floor.** Red bands tighten. | The taut tether is the group-wide alarm. Break line of space now, or commit. |
| STALK (`linked`) | 4 × 8 = 32 | Tethers held taut and arched over the backs; the trio moves as a chain, each body keeping 4–6 m. | A 1.2 m doorway forces the chain into single file — fight them one at a time at the door. |
| CONTACT | 12 | Head dips; the whiskers **bend** against the target and a copper spark shows at the tips. | Back off beyond 1.2 m. The whiskers are its only reach. |
| LUNGE | 12 wind + 8 commit | Both forelimbs draw under, ribs visibly compress, pads grip; the head stays locked. | Sidestep. It cannot steer after the commit. Sever the tether during the wind and it lands wrong. |
| TRIP | 18 wind + 14 sweep | Two bodies brace, the tether **lifts to 0.25 m and vibrates**; the red bands flare. | The span is a visible straight line: step out of it. A step or ledge higher than 0.3 m snags the tether — stairs are safe. Shooting the band during the sweep severs it and **knocks both bodies down** (25-tick stagger). |
| SEVER REACTION | 27 | The body turns to its cut end, whiskers drooping; it touches the copper to the floor repeatedly. | Two free seconds. Punish, or leave. |
| ISOLATED (loop) | 5 × 9 = 45 | Forage: nose down, whiskers trailing, picks up brass and shell casings. Follows at 3 m. Non-hostile unless struck. | Ignore it. It will not block a door. It is the only enemy in the game that eats your litter. |
| HURT (isolated only) | 8 | Whole body flinches, pads skid. | Punish chain. |
| DEATH | 20 | Ribs fold inward, the tether end curls, a last spark. | — |
| CORPSE | 1 | A collapsed rib cage with one lit whisker tip, fading. | — |

### 3.3 How the sheet's details become mechanics

| Sheet detail | Mechanic | Numbers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Porcelain ribs | The ribs are the hit volume; the **gaps between them expose cable** and take 1.25× damage. | Ribs normal, gaps 1.25× |
| Contact whiskers | Its only long-range sense, and destructible. Destroy the fan and that body is blind beyond pad contact: it sweeps its head widely and cannot tag. The group keeps a blinded body within 3 m and stops using it to scout. | Fan 20 HP, one organ |
| Living tether | The network. Sever a spoke: that terminal isolates (state changes, coordination lost, repairs stop). Sever both: the hub is alone. | Hose 60 HP |

### 3.4 Encounter 1 — "The Test" (stratum 0, service gallery)

A linked trio is using the gallery as a route. Its tether is strung across the passage at 1.15 m. Three
non-combat routes, one combat route:

1. **Crouch under the tether.** Native crouch, no new input. The near body's whiskers sweep at crouch
   height — time it, or the whiskers brush you and the trio investigates for four seconds (which is also
   survivable: it decides you are not a conductor).
2. **Shoot a lamp on the far side.** The lamps are on the trio's circuit. The trio re-routes to the dead
   lamp to test the break, ignoring the player for 8–10 s. This is the Circuit's version of a decoy, and it
   is deliberately unlike the Applicant's portrait: a photograph vs a dead circuit.
3. **Pull the junction lever.** Kills the gallery's lights, and all three bodies rush the box, not the
   player, for 12 s. The room goes dark; the player keeps the dark.
4. **Fight.** Best done at the doorway (§3.2, STALK).

This room teaches the tether read with no text: slack tether, taut tether, severed tether, each one a
different animal.

### 3.5 Encounter 2 — "Two trios, one riser" (stratum 0/4 shaft)

Two trios whose tethers cross the lift shaft. Riding the lift **cuts a tether** — the cage severs it at the
band — and leaves two isolated foragers and one functioning pair. Authored, readable, and it uses existing
moving geometry. The severed ends are left sparking in the shaft as a permanent clue.

### 3.6 What it does not do (deliberate limits)

No ceiling-walking beyond authored trays; no teleport; no retarget after a committed lunge; **no electrical
discharge** (that is the Ballast Husk's, and the boss's privilege); **no holding or grabbing the player**
(that is Calmor and the Hem); no learning system — every change of route is authored.

### 3.7 Counters to write into the level

Water (it will not cross; a flooded threshold is a wall to it) · stairs and ledges (trip spans snag) ·
narrow doors (chain bunching) · dead circuits (lamp decoy, lever) · its own severed ends (it returns to
them; a cut end is a lure that works twice).

## 4. Sprite roles to bake

**Bake the body only. Never bake the trio.** Each of the three bodies is its own actor and its own sprite
bank; the tether is engine geometry. All roles use one pivot (world origin) per body; no per-pose
re-centring.

| W | walk | 4 × 7 | 28 | Linked gait; the body leans into the tether. Isolated gait reuses W at a faster tick rate. |
| N | notice | 3 × 7 | 21 | Whiskers snap; ribs lift; head up. |

Framing (see `SPRITE-ROLES.md` for the consolidated table): the body's pose extent is ~1.0 m from the
pivot (whiskers forward), so a **2.0 m span at 768 px** gives 384 px/m and a ~0.9 m body of ~346 px —
close to the Applicant's 326 px. The pipeline's proven 8 m span would render this animal at 86 px and lose
the whiskers; a per-actor span is required. Pivot z = 0.45 m.
