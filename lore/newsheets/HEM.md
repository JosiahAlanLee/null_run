# THE HEM — the woman on the bench

**Status: PROPOSAL throughout.** Sheet: `shared/inputs/nullrun/author-sheets-20261101b/hem.png`.
This is the only one of the three whose **origin I propose as formerly human**, and the only one whose
origin should stay genuinely open (see §2.1 and `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`).

## 1. What the sheet establishes (read off the sheet)

| Sheet detail | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Ready | A standing woman, late thirties or forties, hands together at the chest holding the coat closed. Calm, slight downward look. Nothing monstrous visible. |
| Side / rear | Under the spread coat: many long, segmented limbs, pale wood or bone, jointed like an arachnid's, reaching the floor. The rear view shows the coat hanging over a wide, dark body mass. |
| Windup | Stood wide, hands on the skirt, coat lifted, legs braced. She is showing you the count before it starts. |
| Snare | Sprawled very low and forward, body almost flat, **one limb extended along the floor with the palm open** — the reach is the threat, not the mass. |
| Repaired wool | The red coat is heavy, felted, and mended in many places with visible stitching in mismatched thread. It is the most detailed object on the sheet. |
| Limb root | Where a limb meets the body: a knotted, muscular-sinew socket under the skirt, not a clean socket. It is the only place the limbs look joined rather than grown. |
| Gripping palm | The limb ends in long, articulated fingers with hard knuckle plates: a hand, not a claw. It can grip a floor, a rail, or a person. |
| Ordinary gesture | One hand at the chest, holding the coat closed. Decency. Self-presentation. A person's habit, not a creature's. |

The sheet's whole design is a **person with a coat on**. The limbs are hidden by the coat, the face is
ordinary, and the one gesture that recurs in every view is a woman keeping herself covered.

## 2. Lore proposals

### 2.1 Origin (proposal: formerly human — and the strongest expression of premise 1)

She is not an organism that learned a woman. She is a woman who has been altered, does not consider herself
altered, and has a coat that covers the alteration. That is the author's premise in its plainest form: she
retains personal continuity and has not revised her identity. She knows her legs "are not what they were".
She has opinions about the stairs. She is embarrassed about the state of the hem.

The alternative reading, for the record: a never-human mending organism that assembled a woman's appearance
out of a coat and a habit. I do not recommend it — it would make her a third impression-organism after the
Applicant and the Underprint, and it would spend the best thing about the sheet, which is that the woman is
**a person who is still a person**. Her origin stays open in the production record; my documents never
assert it, they only show her behaving as a person.

### 2.2 What she wants (proposal)

She is waiting for a child. She has a child's red wool coat — size six — that came back down the stair
without the child, and she is mending the coats of everyone who passes so that there will be something to
match when the child comes back up. She has been doing this for a long time and she is not confused about
it; she is *waiting*, which is a decision, not a symptom.

This gives hostility a particular motive, as the handoff asks: she stops people going down. Not out of
malice — because the stair takes things and does not return them, and she has decided that nobody else
goes down while she is sitting there. She will ask you to sit. She will ask you to wait. If you go for the
stair, she gets up.

### 2.3 The coat (proposal, and the reading I would keep)

She will not take the coat off. The institution has asked. The answer is that it is not decent, and that
she is still waiting, and that the coat is hers.

Proposal: **the coat is why she has never been assessed.** The office's form cannot be completed while the
garment is worn; the alteration is therefore incomplete; an incomplete alteration leaves the person
continuous. She has, without knowing the paperwork, kept herself a person by keeping her coat on. This is
one reading of the fictional apocrypha's logic, not a claim about the setting, and the documents state it
only as an administrative fact ("assessment cannot proceed while the coat is worn") without explaining why.

### 2.4 Strata (proposal)

**Primary: stratum 0 waiting rooms and stratum 1 chapel** — anywhere with a bench that faces a stair. She
sits where a person would sit and wait, which is the transit's own furniture and the ossuary's mourners'
benches (the bench that faces the stairwell, which the Keepers' own correspondents complained about).

**Secondary: stratum 2**, a mending room off the scriptorium stacks, where the light is good.

**Absent from the cistern** (wet wool; she will not get the coat wet) and **from the root cathedral** (no
bench, no light, and the stair she is watching does not go there as far as she is concerned). Both absences
are hard boundaries the player can use: the cistern is the one place she cannot follow.

Why she fits the building's logic: the descent is full of people waiting to be assessed. She is the one who
is waiting for something else, in the same posture, on the same bench. She looks exactly like the
institution's own furniture until she does not.

### 2.5 Relationship to the other species

- **Applicant** — the beat I would build a document around. She mended the Applicant's collar once, because
  it was frayed and it stood there so patiently. It now points at the bench where she was sitting and lunges
  at that spot, and she has moved on, so it misses. She is mildly annoyed about it and has not concluded
  that it is not a person. The player, who has seen the Applicant's mechanics, knows more than she does.
- **Circuit** — the mice in the wires. She tells them to keep it down. This is the mundane frame for the
  strangest organism in the set, and it should stay mundane.
- **Underprint** — the damp in the wall by the bench. She has papered it twice and it will not stay flat.
  She does not connect the damp with the pale shape at lean height.
- **Calmor** — professional against domestic restraint. Calmor alters and restrains as a duty; she keeps
  people from the stair as a decision. If they ever meet, Calmor will try to measure her and she will refuse
  to stand up. A memo could carry that.
- **Stone ReDead** — she sits in the chapel with the shelves. She dusts the name on alcove 44 when the
  Keepers are late. She has never asked the occupant to move.

## 3. Encounter design (proposal)

The encounter has four possible shapes, and only one of them is a fight.

### 3.1 Stat block (starting values)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| HP | **150** (demon-class). Head normal; torso under the coat **0.6×**; limbs **1.25×**; limb root **1.5×** |
| Size | 1.7 m standing; 2.4 m wide with the limbs spread; the snare reaches 3.0 m |
| Speed | ordinary 90 u/s; scuttle 240 u/s (0.75× player run) on flat floors; **90 u/s on stairs**; will not enter water |
| Attacks | strike 14 (3-limb flurry, 2.2 m); snare (hold + drag, 3.0 m, 60° cone); no ranged attack |
| Fairness caps | one snare per 6 s; at most two limbs strike at once; the snare cannot start while the player is already held |

### 3.2 Phases and states (35 Hz ticks)

The sheet's named poses map as: `ready` = ORDINARY, `windup` = REVEAL, `snare` = SNARE.

| State | Ticks | Tell (must read without audio) | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ORDINARY (loop) | 6 × 10 = 60 | Seated or standing, sewing, one hand at the chest. **Non-hostile. Nothing about her reads as an enemy.** | Talk, wait, pass, or attack. She does not block the room until you go for the stair. |
| NOTICE | 3 × 8 = 24 | She puts the sewing down, squares it on the bench, and looks up. The coat's hem shifts once, on its own, and settles. | The hem movement is the first honest tell. Back off and she resumes sewing. |
| REVEAL (`windup`) | 5 × 9 = 45 | She stands, grips the skirt with both hands, lifts it, and the limbs **unfold in a fan**, each one finding the floor before the next. She does not hurry. | **Not interruptible.** This is the free-damage window: 1.3 s of committed pose. A prepared player can end the fight here. |
| GAIT | 4 × 8 = 32 | Low scuttle, coat held clear of the floor with one hand. | Stairs. She is slow on them, and the cistern is closed to her. |
| STRIKE | 4 × 4 = 16 | The striking limb's root **bunches** under the skirt before the limb moves; the coat pulls at that point. | Watch the coat, not the limb. Sidestep; the flurry comes in a fixed three-beat rhythm. |
| SNARE (`snare`) | 4 × 5 wind + 3 × 14 hold | The palm opens flat on the floor and the limb lifts and lays itself out along the ground, straight, unhurried. 20 ticks of that, then it **shoots** (8 ticks). | Step out of the 3 m cone, put a column or a step between you, or shoot the **palm** (25 HP) — a broken palm releases the hold and the limb recoils for 22 ticks. |
| HELD | part of SNARE | The fingers close and she **drags you toward her at 2 m/s**, into strike range. | Shooting the palm frees you; so does a wall behind you (the drag stops at geometry). The hold ends by itself after 42 ticks. |
| LIMB RECOIL | 2 × 11 = 22 | The struck limb snaps back; she shifts her weight off it. | Punish the other limbs; the root is exposed for the recoil. |
| HURT | 2 × 5 = 10 | A person's flinch. She does not scream. | — |
| STAND DOWN | 2 × 8 = 16 | If the player leaves the room and stays out for 20 s, she lowers herself, arranges the coat, and picks the sewing back up. | A retreat is a real option and it is honoured. |
| DEATH | 5 × 7 = 35 | The limbs withdraw under the skirt one at a time; she folds down and comes to rest **seated against the bench**, coat arranged over her knees. | — |
| CORPSE | 1 | Seated. Looks like a woman resting. This is the corpse. | — |

### 3.3 How the sheet's details become mechanics

| Sheet detail | Mechanic | Numbers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repaired wool | The coat is her **armour and her refusal**. Torso shots 0.6×; the coat is never destroyed or removed. | 0.6× torso |
| Limb root | The joint under the skirt where the limb is joined, not grown. 1.5×, and it is the only part that reads as *attached*. | 1.5× |
| Gripping palm | The snare's business end, and its weak point. Shoot the palm to break the hold. | 25 HP, 22-tick recoil |
| Ordinary gesture | The non-hostile phase, and the tell that she is calm. When the hand leaves the chest, the encounter has started. | — |
| Invented face | She is the only enemy with a person's face, and it stays a person's face through hurt and death. No transformation, no reveal-face. | — |
| Windup | A committed, uninterruptible reveal. No hidden cancellation. | 45 ticks |

### 3.4 The four shapes of the encounter

1. **Pass her.** She does not stop you if you are not going down the stair. A required route that does not
   use the stair is not blocked (level rule: never gate a required exit on her).
2. **Sit with her.** Stay on the bench for 30 s. She finishes a seam, hands you a mending kit (a small armour
   or durability item — content TBD by Josiah, this is not a systems proposal), and lets you use the stair.
3. **Bring the coat.** The child's red coat exists as a findable object on an ossuary shelf, under a name.
   Returning it is the resolution I find most interesting and the one I refuse to settle: whether she
   leaves, whether she takes her own coat off, or whether she simply stops waiting is **Josiah's decision**
   and is listed in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`. Whatever it is, it should be quiet.
4. **Attack her.** If the player attacks during ORDINARY, she dies as a woman: no reveal, no limbs, the
   skirt falls flat, and the player never learns what was under it. If the player lets the REVEAL complete,
   they fight the Hem. The cheap kill is a real option with a real cost (the room's other content, and one
   document that only appears if she survives).

That last point is the encounter's actual design argument: **the fight is a choice about whether to look.**

### 3.5 What she does not do

No ranged attack; no instant kill; no grab that cannot be broken; no second snare while one is live; no
reveal on death; no monstrous voice; no "she was never human" twist. She is not a boss, and should not be
promoted to one: a boss version would turn a person into a set-piece and lose the only point she makes.

## 4. Sprite roles to bake

One actor, one bank, one pivot. Her **snare pose sets the frame**: the limb extends 3.0 m from the body,
so the span must be at least 6.4 m plus margin. At a **6.8 m span / 768 px** (113 px/m) a 1.7 m body is
~192 px — coarser than the Applicant's 326 px, and the price of the snare. The alternative is a 5.0 m span
with the snare shortened to 2.2 m (body ~261 px); that is a design decision for Josiah, listed in
`OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`.
