# THE UNDERPRINT — the wall that has been leaned on

**Status: PROPOSAL throughout.** Sheet: `shared/inputs/nullrun/author-sheets-20261101b/underprint.png`.
The Underprint was a candidate in §11 of the lore handoff ("plaster, scrim and successive paint layers form
a mobile body… relationship to Applicant unresolved"). This file resolves that relationship as a proposal only.

## 1. What the sheet establishes (read off the sheet)

| Sheet detail | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Ready | A hunched biped, roughly 2.2–2.5 m standing, knuckles near the floor, very long forearms, heavy shoulders. |
| Side / rear | The back is a thick thatch of plaster flakes over dark scrim; the rear view shows no face at all. |
| Detach | The body half-peeled out of a wall, leaving **old floral wallpaper** behind and a heap of flakes at the floor line. |
| Shed | A burst of flakes leaving the **twine-and-scrim frame** exposed, still upright and still moving. |
| Offset impressions | Several faces, at different depths, in the same head: a finished face, and older ones beneath it, misaligned. |
| Plaster and scrim | Chalky layered plates over a woven mesh and twine core. |
| Old wallpaper | Red floral on cream, 1940s-looking, water-stained. It is *behind* the body in the detach panel. |
| Flexible joint | The shoulder and hip are wrapped twine, not plaster — the only places the body bends far. |

The sheet gives **many faces at different depths**, a **wall it came out of**, and **an armature left
standing when the armour is gone**. Those three facts are the creature.

## 2. Lore proposals

### 2.1 Origin (never human — proposal)

The plaster/paint/cloth-scrim material family in the handoff, grown in the accumulated finish layers of a
building that has been repainted, patched and repapered for a century. It began as a patch that accepted
paint. It became, over a long time, a patch that noticed being leaned on.

Its body is made of **repairs**: each plate is a layer of somebody's work. The faces are impressions, not
likenesses — the shape a face leaves in a soft surface, over and over, at different depths. This is the
key thing the sheet shows and the key thing the Applicant does not do.

### 2.2 What it wants (proposal)

To be leaned on, and then to lean back. Its awareness develops in three observable stages, which the level
can show without a word:

1. **A patch that accepts paint.** It is painted over; nothing happens. (Environmental evidence: a wall
   with an unusual number of coats.)
2. **A patch that appears where people lean.** The pale silhouettes in the housekeeping log are at lean
   height, not sitting height, and there are more of them each year. It has learned where the warm weight
   is.
3. **A patch that leans back.** The first contact is not an attack: the wall gives, very slightly, the way
   a crowd gives. The player feels it as a shove; the creature experiences it as answering.

This is the handoff's "increasing awareness is not a ladder to human cognition" made concrete: the
progression is *noticing contact → expecting contact → returning it*, and none of it is a mind.

### 2.3 Strata (proposal)

**Primary: strata 0 and 1** — the transit's waiting rooms, service corridors and stairwells, and the
ossuary's plastered chapel. Its condition is **dry, repeatedly repaired plaster**, which is exactly where
the building's maintenance history is thickest. It is most common at the **seams between strata**, where a
later wall was built against an older one and the finish had to be made to match.

**Absent from the cistern** (water destroys plaster; below the level line there is no plaster left) and
**from the root cathedral** (the cathedral has no plaster of its own, and its pale baffles belong to the
Unaddressed — a distinction that must be kept, or the two creatures blur).

Why it fits the building's logic: every stratum above the cistern is a *repair of the stratum below it*.
The Underprint is the repair layer become an animal. It is the third record-bearing organism in the
bestiary, and the trio is deliberate: the **Erratum** remembers a route, the **Applicant** remembers an
image, the **Underprint** remembers being touched.

### 2.4 Relationship to the Applicant — the non-duplication argument

This is the section the handoff asked for. The two are both pale, layered, face-bearing and plaster-adjacent,
so the differences have to be structural, not cosmetic.

| | Applicant | Underprint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | A **cropped photograph** of one employee | **Impressions** left by many people in soft plaster |
| Face | One face; composed; maintained identically across all states | **Many** faces at different depths; the newest tracks attention, the oldest lags |
| Body logic | Shallow — an image that has not learned depth | Thick — a wall's worth of layers around an armature |
| Attitude to depth | Has none, and lunges because it has none | **Prefers** none: it goes flat against a wall |
| What it wants | To be **looked at**, and to reproduce a pose | To be **leaned on**, and to press back |
| Threat shape | Records your position and commits to it | **Is already in the room**, disguised as its surface |
| Tell | Shutter; a composed face that never changes | Wallpaper pattern breaking; faces turning in sequence, oldest last |
| Counter | Move after the capture; sidestep the lunge | Fight it away from plaster; read the pattern break |

One line for the design file: **the Applicant is a photograph that believes it is a person; the Underprint
is a wall that remembers everyone who leaned on it.** No shared origin is proposed, and none is needed.

### 2.5 Relationship to the other species

- **Erratum** — both preserve a change to the building: the Erratum a route, the Underprint a surface. They
  meet when an Erratum rams an infill: a papered wall can **disgorge an Underprint**. Authored set-piece,
  not simulated. The Erratum is also the only thing in the game that reliably gets an Underprint out of a
  wall without the player having to find the pattern break.
- **Threshold Mimic** — the Mimic *is* the door and cannot leave it; the Underprint *wears* the wall and
  leaves when it wants to. Stationary camouflage vs mobile camouflage. Never give the Underprint a door
  disguise, and never let the Mimic walk.
- **Hem** — she has papered over one, twice, and complains that it will not stay flat.
- **Unaddressed** — same plaster, different organ: the Unaddressed's plaster is a **baffle** (an ear); the
  Underprint's plaster is **skin**. Do not let their materials rhyme.

## 3. Encounter design (proposal)

### 3.1 Stat block (starting values)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| HP | **240 nominal**: three shell layers (60 + 40 + 40 = 140) over a core of **100** |
| Damage taken | shell **0.6×** (40% reduction); core **1.5×**; while flat/concealed **0.5×** (concealed, *not* invulnerable) |
| Effective cost | ~300 raw damage if fought cleanly, less if the shed windows are used. Reference: demon 150, cacodemon 400. |
| Size | 2.2 m standing hunched, 2.5 m at full height, 1.4 m wide at the shoulders |
| Speed | walk 120 u/s; detach burst 300 u/s for 28 ticks; unplated 200 u/s toward the nearest plaster |
| Attacks | swipe 18 (two-hit combo, 2.4 m); press 34, at 1.8 m, **costs the creature 30 of its own HP** and will not be used when the core is below 40; shed burst 3 within 2 m + 25-tick dust |
| Repair | at a plastered wall, 90 ticks to re-plaster one 60-point layer; interrupted by any damage |
| Fairness caps | one press per 8 s; no press while unplated; no re-plaster while the core is exposed and the player has line of sight |

### 3.2 States and durations (35 Hz ticks)

The sheet's named poses map as: `ready` = READY, `detach` = DETACH, `shed` = SHED.

| State | Ticks | Tell (must read without audio) | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| READY (loop) | 4 × 8 = 32 | Hunched, knuckles down. **The faces turn in order**: newest first, oldest last. This is the attention meter, visible at all times. | Read which face is on you; the oldest face always lags, so a fast approach catches it mid-turn. |
| NOTICE | 18 | All faces align; the topmost face stops turning. A fine crack opens along one shoulder. | 0.5 s to break line or commit. |
| WALK | 3 × 9 = 27 | Slow drag, one shoulder low; flakes fall continuously behind it (the flake trail is a tracked decal). | It cannot catch you in the open. Fight it in the open. |
| DETACH | 4 × 11 = 44 | The wallpaper **bubbles outward** in a body shape; the pattern breaks; a pale silhouette stays on the wall when it leaves. | The pale silhouette is a permanent marker: you can see where it was, and where it will go back to. |
| BURST | 3 × 9 = 27 | It comes out of the wall fast, low, arms first, scattering flakes. | It commits to a straight line out of the wall. Stand to the side of the silhouette. |
| FLAT (loop) | 3 × 10 = 30 | It presses into a wall and spreads. Only the **pattern break** and an offset face betray it. | Shoot the wall: paint chips, a pale patch shows, and it flinches — probing a wall with the scattergun is a real tactic. Splash works. |
| SWIPE | 3 × 5 = 15 | One arm rises and the shoulder twine **bunches visibly** before it swings. | Sidestep; the second swing comes back along the same line. |
| PRESS | 5 × 16 = 80 | Both arms raise overhead, the body straightens to full height and the plates on the arms **crack and shed before the blow**. It is spending itself. | Leave 1.8 m, or punish the 25-tick recovery while the arm hangs broken and cannot re-plaster. |
| SHED | 3 × 7 = 21 | A plate layer bursts outward. Dust. Underneath, the **twine frame** is visible and moving. | This is the damage window: 1.5× on everything for 35 ticks. It will run for a wall. |
| RE-PLASTER | 3 × 31 ≈ 90 | It flattens and presses, and the wall's paint **runs toward it** (pigment migrating). | Interrupt with any damage, or stand between it and the wall. |
| HURT | 2 × 5 = 10 | Flinch, flake loss. | — |
| DEATH | 3 × 11 = 33 | The frame folds and the last plate slides off the shoulder; a heap of flakes with a standing twine armature for ~1 s, then that folds too. | — |
| CORPSE | 1 | A heap of painted flakes, one pale face uppermost. | — |

### 3.4 Encounter — "The Papered Room" (stratum 0 waiting room / stratum 1 chapel)

The room's south wall has a **pattern break** at lean height. The room has two exits: the door, and a
second door behind the wallpaper that is painted shut (an old doorway, the Erratum's business). The
Underprint is flat over the old door.

- Ignore it and cross the room quickly: it stays flat. The fight is optional.
- Probe the wall: it flinches, detaches, and fights. The old door is now exposed — a shortcut, at the cost
  of a fight.
- If the player has already met an Erratum, the wall can be opened from the other side.

Rule for the level: **never put a required exit behind an Underprint.** The optional door is optional in
both directions, per the handoff's softlock rule.

### 3.5 What it does not do

No door disguise (Mimic). No walking through walls — detach and attach are authored markers only. No
invulnerability while flat (concealment, 0.5×). No regeneration of the core. No ranged attack. Nothing
that requires the player to see through dust for longer than 25 ticks.
