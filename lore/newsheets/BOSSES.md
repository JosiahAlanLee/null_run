# Two optional sigil/boss ideas

**Status: PROPOSAL. Neither is required, and neither replaces an existing boss.** The live roster rotates
Repetiel, Calmor, Probator and Jurisiel; the boss arena template is a 10–14-gon ring with four radial seal
alcoves. Both ideas below use that template and nothing new in the level format. The Hem is deliberately
**not** here — see the note at the end.

---

## A. CONTINUITY — POST 3 (the Circuit, recommended)

**Why this one.** The Circuit's core idea — one memory in several bodies — is the only one of the three that
scales to a boss without inventing a new species. The fight's question ("which body is the memory?") is
answered by the anatomy the sheet already gives: the hub with the split root.

**The body.** A porcelain switchboard torso, 2.4 m tall, mounted on a cable trunk rooted in the arena
floor. Six tethers leave it in three pairs, each pair ending in a terminal body of the ordinary Circuit
species. The core's ribs are the switchboard's own porcelain panels; when they open, the interior is a
copper coil with no organic tissue at all.

**Fight shape (starting values).**

| Phase | Live terminals | Core state | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 6 | sealed, invulnerable | The trio logic runs at boss scale: trips sweep the whole ring, contact tags relay, one lunge at a time. |
| 2 | 4 | cage opens, **arcs** | The core gets the one electrical discharge in the species: a 20-damage arc to a tagged player, 1.5 s cooldown. |
| 3 | ≤2 | exposed, slow | The trunk drags the core around the dais; 30-damage slam, long recovery. |
| Death | 0 | opens like a panel | The arena lights go out a pair at a time, in the order the pairs died. |

- Core HP **700**; terminals 90 each (540). Reference: baron 1000. Total raw ~1,240 if every terminal is
  killed and the core is fought; less if the player severs instead.
- **Severing is the boss's skill route:** a severed terminal is isolated and non-hostile, and the core's
  invulnerability counts *live* terminals — so cutting tethers in the right order opens the core faster than
  killing bodies. That is the mechanic the species was designed around, at boss scale.
- Fairness: no more than one trip in any 3 s; arcs only hit a tagged player and are telegraphed by the
  core's ribs brightening for 20 ticks; the core never moves in phase 1.

**Sigil.** Three interlocked rings in a chain, each broken by a red tick, one break wider than the others.
The works department stencils it on cable tags, junction-box doors and test sheets; the arena's four seal
alcoves carry three rings and, in the fourth, the break mark alone.
**In the fight:** each alcove plaque lights when its pair is live. The fourth never lights. Shooting a lit
plaque severs that pair — the sigil is the switchboard, and the mark on the wall is the control.

**Diegesis (my favourite part).** The arena's entry plate reads `CONTINUITY — POST 3`. The office's
personnel file for the post names an employee who was never on any roster and who has no intake number.
The office cannot file a non-person, so it filed a vacancy and the vacancy has been filled for forty years.

**Cost.** Six extra actors, a group controller, an engine-side tether per spoke, and one new attack (arc)
that must not be confused with the Ballast Husk's. That is a real integration job — but it is the same job
as the ordinary Circuit encounter, done once, so the boss is cheap *after* the species works.

## B. THE PAPERED ROOM (the Underprint, later)

**Why this one.** It is the most distinctive idea in the set and the most expensive. A room whose four
papered walls are a single organism: the player fights it as architecture.

**Fight shape.**

1. **The room watches.** Two ordinary Underprints detach and fight. The walls are flat and cannot be hurt;
   the wallpaper pattern's one offset petal moves when the player is not looking at it.
2. **The room sheds.** All four walls burst at once. A 4 m Underprint steps out of the south wall, leaving a
   **body-shaped absence in the wallpaper** — the absence is the room's second exit, and it is the only way
   out that is not the door.
3. **The room is fought.** The giant has no re-plaster source; instead it takes plates from whichever wall
   the player is not facing, so the arena literally runs out of wall. When the last wall is bare, the
   armature is exposed and the fight ends.

**Sigil.** A four-petal floral with one petal offset by 30°. It repeats on the wallpaper at 61 cm and is
stamped on the room's door. **In the fight:** the offset petal marks the live panel — the room's own pattern
is the tell. (The same mark appears on the wallpaper sample in `doc-p03`.)

**Diegesis.** The room schedule calls it `14A (damp)`. The decorating requisitions for it are the only ones
in the file that have never been closed.

**Cost and risk.** Needs a bespoke 12 m-span bake or a non-sprite body, a dust/visibility effect, four
destructible wall panels, and a room whose exit appears mid-fight (the handoff's softlock rule applies: the
door must work before and after). It is a set-piece for later, not a first proof.

---

## Recommendation

Build **A** first, and only after the ordinary Circuit encounter has passed a native play test. **B** is the
better idea and the worse first project; hold it until there is a room-scale boss pipeline.

**The Hem is not boss material.** A boss version would turn a person into a set-piece and lose the only
point she makes — that she is a woman waiting on a bench, and that the fight is a choice about whether to
look. If she recurs, she should recur as the same woman, in the same coat, on a different bench.
