# MATERIALS — what the descent yields, how you take it, how you carry it

**Status: PROPOSAL.** Text only. Numbers are prototype starting values. Rarity legend: **C** common,
**U** uncommon, **R** rare, **1** one per run.

## 1. The core rule: HOW you kill decides WHAT you harvest

The kill method is the harvest method. A surgical kill leaves organic material; a brutal kill leaves
mechanical. The player chooses which by choosing the weapon and the aim point — and pays the risk of the
surgical kill (the core is 0.25 m across, behind rib bars, and the controlled Armature is already a panic
spike).

| Kill method (class) | What it does to the body | What the remains yield |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SHEAR** — Fathom Hook | cuts limbs/bars while it lives; the heap keeps whole, straight bars | rebar (cut); shearing the control tendon yields tendon and frees the host (per ECOLOGY) |
| **PIERCING** — Registrar, Comptroller | through the joints, tie wire snaps; through the green core, the colony shatters | tie wire; colony glass + tendon (core-fouled scrap) |
| **BLUNT** — Transit-12, Plumb Bombard | batters the rebar cage until it unbinds; bars bend and break | rebar (bent) |
| **RESONANCE** — Hymnodyne, Aethometer | rattles the bindings loose; the cage unspools, the wires shake free | tie wire (loose coils); bars are ruined (no rebar yield) |

So: **core shot → organic. Cage-batter → mechanical.** A controlled Armature killed by cage-batter yields
rebar and wire but the colony glass is shattered (lost); killed by the core shot it yields colony glass and
tendon but the bindings were eaten by the colony (no wire). You cannot have both from one body. The heap
records the choice (a core-fouled scrap heap reads differently from a battered heap — see ARMATURE death
rules).

**The harvest action.** Loot is physical (PILLARS §1): kneel at the heap or corpse, **2 s per unit**,
cut/pry with the hook or a tool. Harvesting is loud — a clatter that wakes packs within **12 m** (heaps
already wake the nearest pack within 6 m when stepped on; taking from them is worse). The material appears
in your hand, not your inventory; you walk it to your satchel, or you leave it. No vacuum, no float.

## 2. Carrying: satchel, case, pockets, hands

- **Issue Satchel** (the native backpack, per AMMO-PICKUPS): canvas and leather, the broken strap knotted
  back together. **12 slots / 12 kg**, found in transit stores.
- **Stores Case**: a flat steel case from a service room, **8 slots / 20 kg**. Occupies one hand while
  carried; opening it is a physical act (lid, 1 s, a clack). You can put it down — and lose it, or come
  back for it. Carried at a run, it bangs on railings (noise +1).
- **Pockets**: 4 slots, small items only (≤ 0.5 kg: wire, shards, fibre, emulsion, seals).
- **Hands**: one heavy thing each (case, crate, valve, bronze plate).
- Nothing despawns mid-stratum; what you drop stays where you dropped it (heaps persist per the Armature
  room rules).

**Encumbrance** (total carried weight; baseline run 320 u/s):

| Load | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| ≤ 14 kg | normal. |
| 14–20 kg | run 288 u/s (−10%), weapon raise +15%, footstep noise +1. |
| 20–26 kg | run 256 u/s (−20%), sprint capped at 1 s, footsteps wake Armatures at 10 m instead of 8. |
| > 26 kg | run 224 u/s, no sprint, aim drifts while moving. |

The Load Frame upgrade raises every threshold by +16 kg. The decision "take the case or leave it" is real:
the case holds more than the satchel, and costs you a hand, a slower run and a louder approach. The PILLARS
line — a heavy load slows you — is a rule, not a flavour line.

## 5. Where materials go (one-line map)

- **UPGRADE (workbench):** rebar, tie wire, brass, porcelain, cable, valve, scaffold tube, limestone,
  bronze cradle/plate, tube glass, felt wadding, cast iron, gauge brass, ballast core, porcelain contact,
  lagging plate, counterweight block, seal wax (minor).
- **MUTATION (nursery tray / spore bed):** colony glass, tendon, chitin, membrane, basin residue,
  root growth, Hollow fibre, photoform emulsion, dry fibre, bone, amber shard, white-bloom filament,
  louvre glass, paper emulsion (minor).
- **Both:** bone (braces the Load Frame as well as the marrow), cable (feeds mods and the crest).
- **Ammo as material:** two recipes — Capacitor Forearms and the Shoulder Lamp — ask for a **lamp cell**.
  The cell is ammunition, and the bench does not care: it is a material if you are willing to shoot one
  fewer shot. Deliberate; flagged in NOTES.

A node recipe names its materials; a satchel full of rebar is a decision about which tree you are feeding.
The two trees do not just cost differently — they eat differently, from the same rooms.
