# BODY-BUDGET — one body, two claims on it

**Status: PROPOSAL.** Text only. This is the Shadowrun-Essence layer: a shared pool both trees spend, a
body with slots they compete for, and rules for what happens when iron and growth try to share the same
limb.

## 1. The pool: INTEGRITY, 20 points

- Every node — upgrade or mutation — costs **2 Integrity**. Ten nodes maximum, from either tree or mixed.
- Integrity is *room*, not health. Reaching 0 means the body is full, not that anything is lost. There is
  no "essence death": in the Purgatorio the price is lived (pain, drift, weight), not spent as a soul.
  The Shadowrun echo is structural only — augmentation costs something, and both kinds draw on the same
  body.
- What differs is what else each tree takes: upgrades take **pain, weight, noise, heat and maintenance**;
  mutations take **drift, oddities and time** (rooting). The budget is shared; the taxes are not.

## 2. The slots: twelve named places

One node per slot, and each slot shows. The slot table is in `TREES.md` §5; the body map is:

forearms · hands · legs · feet · torso · spine · head · eyes · ears · skin · gut · voice

A forearm is either a capacitor or laced with tendon. A voice is either a relay or a root-tongue. The
slots are what make the two trees *opposed* rather than merely parallel: you cannot take both answers to
the same problem, because they live in the same place.

## 3. The refusal line: seven nodes of one tree shuts the other

- **7 upgrades** (14 Integrity): the nursery tray goes cold — growth will not root into a machine. The
  materials sit inert in the medium. Upgrades remain available.
- **7 mutations**: the workbench refuses you — the vice will not close, the light stays dim, and the
  stencil now reads `NOT PERSONNEL`. Mutations remain available.
- Below seven each way, mixing is free. The final nodes unlock at six of a tree; taking the final node
  does **not** lock the other tree, but the seventh node of that tree does. This is the structural
  anti-snowball: mono-builds are allowed to finish their road, and the road is a dead end.

## 4. Mixing rules: rejection, acceptance, synergy

- **Same slot: hard refusal.** The bench sparks and stops; the tray will not take. In-world: *the part is
  already there.* You cannot install a Boiler-Plate over the Colony's Rhythm, or bed Quiet Soles onto
  Hydraulic Lock spurs.
- **Same limb, different slots** (forearm + hand on one arm; leg + foot on one leg): allowed, but the pair
  runs at **−25% effect each**, with a visible symptom — a tremor at the joint, pale seepage where steel
  meets growth, noise +1. This is the brief's line made mechanical: *rebar and fungus don't share a forearm
  well.* The bench and the tray both warn first (the bench stencil: `DO NOT SHARE A LIMB`; the tray's
  paper label lists the pairs that fail). Deterministic, no roll, no destroyed materials — the cost is the
  penalty, and you can see it coming.
- **Different limbs: no penalty.**
- **Synergies** (specific mixed pairs that work better together; these are the design's reward for the
  balanced path):
  1. **Shoulder Lamp (HEAD-up) + Fog Eyelids (EYES-mut) — "the lamp in the fog."** Your lamp no longer
     blooms in fog; you get hands-free light and clear fog sight together.
  2. **Coolant Sheath (SKIN-up) + Basin Respiration (GUT-mut) — "heat and damp."** The wheeze is gone
     (the sheath cools the breath) and resonance overheat stays halved.
  3. **Hydraulic Lock (FEET-up) + Tendon Lacing (FOREARMS-mut) — "the anchored fisherman."** While
     planted, hook swings cost no stagger and +25% damage on the next one.
  4. **Crest Filaments (HEAD-mut) + Boiler-Plate (TORSO-up) — "the patient butcher."** After you survive
     a controlled Armature's two-arm slam, its core is outlined for 1 s: one guided shot.
  5. **Load Frame (SPINE-up) + Quiet Soles (FEET-mut) — "the quiet porter."** The frame's creak is
     dampened at a walk (−1 noise); you carry heavy and still step soft.

Synergies are cross-region by construction; same-limb pairs can never be synergies, which is what makes
the rejection rule readable: near things conflict, far things compound.

## 5. The ends: what pushing all the way down one tree means

### 5.1 THE WORKS BODY (final node, 6+ upgrades)

The descent files you. Effects: maintenance ends (the body schedules itself); noise −2 (it moves like
machinery that was meant to be there); **never-human organisms treat you as equipment** — free Armatures
ignore you entirely unless you attack (they test loads, not machines), and the Spindle now sees you as a
host: colony cores can lodge in **you** (a new, personal version of the infection loop — the rise is yours
to fear). Inhabitants treat you as office property: Calmor tries to maintain you, the Surveyor surveys
you, doors open for you and no longer need a second push.

**The ending.** You finish the descent; at the desk, the intake form is rendered:
`CLASS: EQUIPMENT. DISPOSITION: SURPLUS — IN SERVICE.` The exit door lists you. You do not leave.

### 5.2 THE DRIFT (final node, 6+ mutations)

Drift becomes 12 and settles. Effects: **creatures and inhabitants treat you as a resident** — nothing
attacks you unprovoked; the ReDead walks beside you; Calmor offers to alter your coat; the Visitor offers
his hand and is gentle; the Unaddressed stops testing you and simply answers. Office systems stop
recognising you: turnstiles stand still, the benches face you, the Hymnodyne's keys feel like someone
else's teeth.

**The ending.** `CLASS: RESIDENT. DISPOSITION: DO NOT ATTEMPT EVICTION.` The turnstile will not open.
You finish the descent as one of them, and stay.

### 5.3 The balanced end (no final node)

You reach the bottom as personnel: `CLASS: PERSONNEL. DISPOSITION: ASSESSED — CLEARED TO LEAVE.` The run's
win. You leave carrying the aches and the drift you chose, and the file remembers both.

### 5.4 The Purgatorio reading

The extreme paths are not punishments for power; they are disciplines carried too far, and the game tells
the player so before it happens (the bench stencil, the tray's label, the file's marginalia — see
`RUN-STRUCTURE.md`). Suffering is chosen and borne either way (PILLARS §4); the difference is what you
stop being. The descent lets you go all the way down either road — and the top will not take you back
down the other. The middle is not safe; it is simply still a person's road.

## 6. DRIFT thresholds (the mutation meter, 0–12)

| Drift | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | oddities only (TREES §4 table) |
| 3–4 | machines hesitate: turnstile 1 s, doors need a second push; bench installs take +3 s |
| 5–7 | the office notices: intake forms about you appear in the world; the Surveyor includes you in a traverse once per stratum; the ReDead walks with you in the ossuary |
| 8+ | **not personnel**: the bench refuses you if seven mutation nodes have not already closed it (whichever comes first — no new upgrades, no maintenance); at the top station the turnstile will not open — the RESIDENT ending is set |
| 12 | the Drift's final state (§5.2) |

Pain, noise, heat and encumbrance (the upgrade taxes) live in `TREES.md` §2 and `MATERIALS.md` §2; drift
is the mutation tree's half of the same body.
