# STRATA — competing architectural histories of the five layers

**Status: PROPOSAL for Josiah to accept or reject.** These histories deliberately disagree with one another, the way the setting's records should. No single account below is the truth; where one account is marked "closest to observable fact", that only means the layer's materials in the current game support it. Nothing here establishes a shared cause, and nothing here makes a never-human organism a former person or vice versa.

Observable ground rules the histories must respect (from current game work):
- Stratum 0 is fluorescent, newest, service-circulation; stratum 4 is cable-and-rebar roots grown through masonry. Lighting descends as you descend. (Established by the depth themes; names below match `depths/themes.py`.)
- The five strata are stacked in one shaft-like descent: transit → ossuary → scriptorium → cistern → root cathedral.

---

## 0. INSTITUTIONAL TRANSIT — "the top station"

**Built by (proposal):** the Null Run Receiving Office (NRRO), Works & Descent division, in the era of the fluorescent fitting. The office itself may postdate the layers below it and merely *claims* them.

**Built for (proposal):** to move paper, personnel and assessment cases down into the works; platforms, turnstiles, a waiting room whose benches face the stairwell, service doors that only open one way. The institutional reading: the transit is a receiving hall — people arrive, are processed, and descend for assessment.

**What the next stratum's builders got wrong (proposal):** the Shelf-Keepers' Order, builders of the ossuary, kept a ledger calling the transit **"the corpse line."** Their record claims the platforms were built to lower the dead gently — that the turnstiles were a mourners' gate, that the fluorescent light was "candlelight done in tubes", and that the waiting room's benches were bier-rests. They found the one-way doors proof: the living enter; what returns is not the living. The transit's own signage says *passengers*; the ossuary ledger says *the last passengers*. Both cannot be right. Both are written as if the other account is a forgery.

**Records at variance:** transit timetable chits vs. ossuary shelf-marks that reuse the same numbers. A painted platform arrow points DOWN and has been re-lettered three times: "DESCENT", "INTERMENT", "DESCENT" again.

## 1. LIMESTONE OSSUARY — "the dry galleries"

**Built by (proposal):** the Shelf-Keepers' Order, a funerary congregation that worked the limestone before the office existed (their own claim) or that was itself a predecessor department of the office (the office's claim). They shelved the dead in dry galleries because they believed stone that stays dry keeps the name legible.

**Built for (proposal):** the orderly storage of remains; every alcove is a shelf, every shelf a name, every name kept dusted and turned toward the lamp. The current "former occupants" reading comes from here: the order's own plaques call the alcoves occupied, and the modern intake forms have never corrected the verb.

**What the next stratum's builders got wrong (proposal):** the Bronze Margins, builders of the scriptorium, concluded the ossuary was a **library with a binding problem**. They read the shelf-bones as pressed pages, the names as catalogue entries, and the dust as a preservative. They therefore built the scriptorium directly below "to continue the copying," and began transcribing the shelves — assigning shelf-marks, numbering skulls as folios, binding loose remains in bronze cradles "for the comfort of the text." Their error survives in the current office's filing: ossuary residents are still logged as "volumes".

**Records at variance:** the ossuary's own register says the order buried the dead to keep them *from being copied*. The scriptorium's catalogue says the ossuary was the first scriptorium and its keepers merely lost the art of reading. The office's archaeology memo (NRRO-DOC-311) says the two buildings were built in the opposite order, and the stair between them "may not exist in one of the two accounts."

## 2. BRONZE SCRIPTORIUM — "the bronze margins"

**Built by (proposal):** the Bronze Margins, a copying and legal congregation. They worked in bronze because bronze, unlike paper, "does not forget its first reading" — their plates are cast, then corrected by overstriking, so every room carries at least two layers of text.

**Built for (proposal):** transcription, custody of names, and the settlement of disputes by precedent. Desks face the walls because the walls are the record; the inscriptions follow the walls, and the oldest line is always at the door so a reader enters a case already in progress. The office's current forms descend from Margin plates: the intake record's boxes, the redaction block, the "awaiting assessment" footer all have bronze ancestors.

**What the next stratum's builders got wrong (proposal):** the Descent Works, builders of the cistern, read the bronze lattice as **drainage scaffold** — they took the inscription channels for conduits, the overstruck corrections for solder runs, and the desk gutters for spillways. Their engineers rerouted water "back where it belonged" and drowned the reading floor. The cistern's own dedication plate thanks the Margins for "the excellent pipework." The scriptorium, from below the water, calls this the greatest misreading in the history of the descent: *they watered the names.*

**Records at variance:** a Margin plate says the flood was deliberate — "we were read too slowly." A Descent Works memo says the flood was an accident of pressure, and the plate is a later forgery by a wet hand. The water level in the game sits between the two accounts: high enough to cover the desks, low enough to leave the highest line of text dry.

## 3. FLOODED CISTERN — "the level line"

**Built by (proposal):** Descent Works, a works department that may have been the NRRO's own predecessor or a separate authority the office later absorbed. Their style is the tank: brick cores, cast grates, gauges, one measured line of stillness.

**Built for (proposal):** water storage and pressure — the official reason — or (the Margin plate's reason) the deliberate drowning of the scriptorium. Every surface carries the level line: the water has been at the same height long enough that the tile above and below it are two different colours, and the line itself is the only clean ring in the room.

**What the next stratum's builders got wrong (proposal):** the construction organisms of the root cathedral — never human, growing toward awareness through material — misread the cistern completely and productively. They took the level line for a **floor**, the water for a slow record, the pipes for limbs and the reflections for instructions. Their growth "corrects" the cistern's plan: they have built (grown) a staircase of cable where the cistern's own stair was removed, and their roots descend to exactly the level line and stop, the way a reader stops at the end of a paragraph. The office classifies this as subsidence; the organisms' own pattern — visible in the game as the cable stairs and the root ceiling — says otherwise.

**Records at variance:** Descent Works logged the cistern as full and stable for the whole period its logs cover. The organisms' growth marks show three distinct water heights, each lower than the last, each crossed out. The office's gauge is still painted at the highest mark.

## 4. ROOT CATHEDRAL — "the grown layer"

**Built by (proposal):** not built. Grown, by never-human construction organisms — cable and rebar roots, masonry that became organism, vaults that are ribs rather than arches. If it has a builder, the builder is the material itself learning a room.

**Built for (proposal):** no purpose that was ever written down. The best observable account: the layer *imitates* the layers above it the way a colony imitates what it touches — it has bays because the transit had bays, shelves because the ossuary had shelves, a reading line because the scriptorium had one, and a still pool because the cistern had one. The cathedral is the descent remembering itself.

**What the next stratum's builders got wrong:** there is no next stratum. Instead, **every layer above got the cathedral wrong, and each wrongness is a competing architectural history:**
- The Shelf-Keepers called it a fossil forest and laid out their galleries "to match its plan," which is why ossuary niches and root bays share a module.
- The Bronze Margins called the roots bound manuscript wire and the vault ribs "an unrolled scroll," and cast their lattice to its measure.
- Descent Works called it fungal contamination and pressure damage, and built the cistern's grates "to keep the growth out" — the grates the growth has since passed through.
- The NRRO's current intake calls it **unstable structure** and files it under Works & Descent, not under Biology, Persons, or Records. The office cannot admit what it houses; the cathedral is the clearest case.

**Records at variance:** every layer's account of the cathedral describes a different room. The Shelf-Keepers saw columns; the Margins saw lines of text; the Works saw pipes; the office sees damage. The only record that matches the room as built is none of them: it is the room itself, which grows slightly toward each observer, and which the Unaddressed consortium is apparently trying to read.
