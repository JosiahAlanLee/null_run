# BESTIARY — twelve sheets, two voices

**Status: PROPOSAL for Josiah to accept or reject.** Each entry pairs two voices: a dry, partially redacted intake record from the invented Null Run Receiving Office (NRRO), and an oblique found-text fragment in item-description style. The two voices may disagree; neither is authoritative. **Body rules are kept as written** from the author materials and the "Material and author authority" section. Origin assignments remain open where the handoff leaves them open, and the intake's classification is the *office's* opinion, not a fact about the creature.

Established premises preserved throughout: former-human continuity goes unrecognised by its subjects; the three new organisms were never human. Encounter timings quoted from the briefs are prototype starting values, not accepted balance.

Covered: the twelve creatures with local sheets. **Hollow is not covered** — its new sheet is a private library link that was not supplied to this machine, and this worker must not claim to have seen it (see `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`).

Form key used in intake records: `NR-███-X` subject number, `Class`, `Station`, `Description`, `Behaviour`, `Origin`, `Notes`, `Disposition`. `██` marks redaction.

## 1. Calmor — restraint attendant

**NRRO INTAKE NR-144-C — ATTENDANT (RESTRAINT)**
- Subject no.: NR-144-C. Received ██.██.19██. Class: ATTENDANT, restraint grade. Station: stratum 2, bronze scriptorium, reading floor west.
- Description: massive, stooped build; flared rib structure; acoustic padding at the chest and forearms; hands disproportionately large; head carried low; garment of stitched felt and hide. Subject reports the fit is "close but improving."
- Behaviour: performs restraint and alteration duties without being scheduled. Handles small objects with visible difficulty; has been observed replacing a cuff, a clasp, and the same latch three times in one shift. When a procedure is interrupted, subject restrains the interruption. Subject describes this as helping.
- Origin: subject self-identifies as staff. Whether the subject was ever on the roster is UNRESOLVED. Do not resolve on site.
- Notes: do not accept fittings from subject without inspection. Do not permit subject to measure personnel.
- Disposition: AWAITING ASSESSMENT. Do not release.

**Found text (stitched, felt tag, reading floor):**
> "Alterations for a shape I no longer — no, for a shape I am still getting used to. The needles are too small now. That is not my fault. If I hold someone still long enough, they stop being cold. I am told that is what helping is."

## 2. Repetiel — double-faced courier

**NRRO INTAKE NR-101-R — COURIER (DOUBLE-DUTY)**
- Subject no.: NR-101-R. Received ██.██.19██. Class: COURIER. Station: all five strata; observed most often on the transit platforms and the scriptorium stairs.
- Description: lean build; two faces; integrated laryngeal tube running the length of the throat and returning on itself; ivory shell across the shoulders; dark fibres at the joints; long limbs; a return-loop seal at the tube's junction.
- Behaviour: delivers and returns messages. When the second mouth speaks, the subject pauses and blames the acoustics of whichever room it is in. The interruption is audible to staff in some rooms and to nobody in others.
- Origin: subject self-identifies as a courier with a route. Route records for the subject do not exist. UNRESOLVED.
- Notes: messages returned by subject may bear two answers. File both.
- Disposition: AWAITING ASSESSMENT.

**Found text (delivery slip, both sides signed, ossuary landing):**
> "Spoke twice this morning. The echo answered first. I have been putting the reply in the wrong bag for years, and the bags do not mind. Ask me again on the stairs, where the acoustics are honest."

## 3. Scriptorax — six-armed archivist

**NRRO INTAKE NR-112-S — ARCHIVIST (SENIOR)**
- Subject no.: NR-112-S. Received ██.██.19██. Class: ARCHIVIST. Station: stratum 2, bronze scriptorium, south stacks.
- Description: emaciated build; six arms; a coat assembled entirely from sleeves; each sleeve bears a numbered strip; seams drawn taut; deep hood. The two central hands work with precision; the four peripheral hands grasp or drag what the central hands set aside.
- Behaviour: retrieves and re-shelves records without a key. Numbered strips change between observations. When asked about a particular sleeve, subject gives the name of a colleague; on a later visit the same sleeve carries a different name, and the subject gives the first name again.
- Origin: subject self-identifies as staff of long standing. Whether the sleeves were ever attached to other persons is UNRESOLVED.
- Notes: do not unpick seams. Do not offer a seventh sleeve.
- Disposition: AWAITING ASSESSMENT.

**Found text (numbered strip, folded in a stack):**
> "SLEEVE 6. Return to Room 6. Room 6 has been re-numbered Room 6. The coat remembers colleagues the way a room remembers its old number. I keep the spares because someone will come back for them, and I will know them by the sleeve."

## 4. Choraz — sixteen-horn herald

**NRRO INTAKE NR-119-C — HERALD (BROADCAST)**
- Subject no.: NR-119-C. Received ██.██.19██. Class: HERALD. Station: stratum 2, bronze scriptorium, reading floor east; also reported on ossuary stairs.
- Description: substantial, broad-backed build; sixteen trumpet growths rooted into the spine and shoulders; empty helmet worn at all times; stance planted and braced before sounding.
- Behaviour: proclaims. The voice carries poorly at distance; subject compensates by leaning closer to the listener. Personnel are advised that proximity does not improve audibility, only pressure. When the proclamation is repeated, it is repeated louder and nearer.
- Origin: subject self-identifies as an appointed herald. The office that would have made the appointment is not on any roster. UNRESOLVED.
- Notes: do not answer a proclamation. An answer is treated as an audience.
- Disposition: AWAITING ASSESSMENT.

**Found text (stencil, proclamation ending mid-word, transit wall):**
> "BY AUTHORITY VESTED IN THE EMPTY HELMET, THE HOUR IS ANNOUNCED: THE HOUR IS —" (the stencil is worn through; beneath it, scratched by hand: "the hour is the same as yesterday. I checked. Why does nobody believe me.")

## 5. Stone ReDead — ossuary resident

**NRRO INTAKE NR-077-D — RESIDENT (OSS. GALLERY)**
- Subject no.: NR-077-D. Received ██.██.19██. Class: RESIDENT. Station: stratum 1, limestone ossuary, north gallery, alcove 44.
- Description: gaunt humanoid; cracked limestone mask over the upper face; dry sinew; slate cloth; long grasping hands. When the mask is lifted, subject shrieks and prepares to seize; personnel are not to lift the mask. Subject does not respond to its shelf-name.
- Behaviour: remains in or near alcove 44 unless disturbed; will follow at a walk and reach rather than run. Grip is close and difficult to break; a stagger follows if the subject is made to release.
- Origin: alcove 44's shelf-mark names a person; subject does not answer to it. Whether subject *is* that person, *keeps* that person, or is shelved beside that person is UNRESOLVED.
- Notes: keep the name dusted. Do not re-shelve the subject.
- Disposition: RESIDENT. Do not attempt eviction.

**Found text (scratched into the limestone shelf, beside the name):**
> "The name is still here. I checked the name and the name checked back. They keep dusting it so it stays legible, and as long as it stays legible I know which shelf is mine. Do not read it aloud. Reading aloud is how they take the next one down."

## 6. Surveyor — the line

**NRRO INTAKE NR-066-S — INSTRUMENT (SURVEY)**
- Subject no.: NR-066-S. Received ██.██.19██. Class: INSTRUMENT. Station: all five strata; most recently stratum 3, flooded cistern, causeway.
- Description: tense, instrument-bodied; optical gimbal at the head; survey shoes; articulated supports; cable tendons; degree rings engraved on the chest plate and forearms.
- Behaviour: acquires an aim and holds it before discharging along a fixed line. The aim is announced by the gimbal; personnel who see the gimbal settle should leave the line. Subject resurveys the same traverse repeatedly and marks discrepancies.
- Origin: UNRESOLVED. The office files the subject as equipment on long loan; the loan paperwork names a lender no department admits to.
- Notes: a third support leg appears in the latest art-direction studies; it is a drawing, not yet a recorded fact of the subject. Do not log it.
- Disposition: IN SERVICE. Do not recall.

**Found text (survey chalk, cistern causeway, boxed):**
> "Line 7 ends at the wall. The wall was not there when Line 7 was run. I have re-run Line 7 eleven times and the wall moves one mark closer each time. I am not wrong. The room is being corrected, and the room is not correcting toward my drawing."

## 7. Vore Spindle — spore-bearer

**NRRO INTAKE NR-091-V — VERMIN (SPORE-BEARING)**
- Subject no.: NR-091-V. Received ██.██.19██. Class: VERMIN. Station: stratum 3, flooded cistern; reported on the islands and beneath the grates.
- Description: low three-limbed organism; hook joints; pale chitin; abdomen suspended between the limbs; launch ribs that open before discharge; a faceted spore held in membrane.
- Behaviour: telegraphs, then launches a bounded homing spore. The spore curves within a fixed envelope and can be outrun or outcornered. Personnel report the launch sound carries further than the spore does.
- Origin: UNRESOLVED. Pest-control ledger lists the subject under "eggs, unattributable."
- Notes: do not count the spores twice. The count never comes out the same.
- Disposition: CONTAINED LOCALLY. Do not transport.

**Found text (pest-control ledger page, cistern pump room):**
> "Third count: fourteen. Second count: eleven. First count: nine. The missing ones are being put back by something that does not want the shelf full. I have stopped counting and started listening: the water counts them for me, one splash at a time, and the water is never wrong."

## 8. Ballast Husk — wire shambler

**NRRO INTAKE NR-138-B — EQUIPMENT (DAMAGED)**
- Subject no.: NR-138-B. Received ██.██.19██. Class: EQUIPMENT. Station: stratum 0, institutional transit, service corridors and the lower platforms.
- Description: heavy, asymmetric cable-muscle build; ballast cores at the shoulders and hip; porcelain contacts; rusted frame; electrical maw recessed in the chest mass.
- Behaviour: charges along a straight arc when engaged; arcs discharge from the maw. The charge is loud and the subject does not corner well; cover and lateral movement are the recommended countermeasures. Subject has never been observed operating, only *operated*.
- Origin: filed as a hoist winch gone missing from Stores. The winch was recovered in Stores. UNRESOLVED.
- Notes: the subject hums in two keys when nobody is near. Do not tune it.
- Disposition: DO NOT RECONNECT.

**Found text (shift log, transit service room, ballpoint on carbon):**
> "Night shift. The winch hummed again, low E and something above it, like it was remembering a song that has words. I stood close to listen and it stopped, embarrassed. Machinery does not get embarrassed. I have requested a transfer to day shift on the grounds that day shift has more people and fewer songs."

## 9. Threshold Mimic — the false exit

**NRRO INTAKE NR-041-T — DOOR (NON-STANDARD)**
- Subject no.: NR-041-T. Received ██.██.19██. Class: DOOR. Station: stratum 0 and stratum 1, door 44, transit east wall; reported at other frames since.
- Description: an ordinary service door in all respects at rest: leaf, handle, rubber seals, painted frame. Behind the disguise: fixed muscular jaws, concrete teeth, seals serving as lips, roots running into the wall cavity.
- Behaviour: stationary. Gives a brief warning before the bite; the reach is short. The door does not walk, does not follow, and cannot be propped open by force of wedges. Personnel are advised to treat every door that should not exist as a door that does not exist.
- Origin: wall-cavity roots; the cavity's survey predates the building. UNRESOLVED.
- Notes: door schedule lists door 44 as "installed, never ordered."
- Disposition: SEALED, NOT REMOVED.

**Found text (chit, taped to the frame, transit east wall):**
> "This door is a door. If it is not a door, do not tell it. It has waited this long to be opened and it can wait longer. The last crew to wedge it wrote their names on the handle. The handle has kept them."

## 10. The Applicant — photoform colony

**Established: never human.** A construction organism that learned a face from a cropped personnel photograph before it learned depth, legs, or the difference between a picture and a person.

**NRRO INTAKE NR-152-A — APPLICANT (PENDING)**
- Subject no.: NR-152-A. Received ██.██.19██. Class: APPLICANT. Station: stratum 0, institutional transit, waiting room; subject sits where applicants sit.
- Description: two arms, two legs; each leg carries two sequential opposed knee hinges. Face is a shallow emulsion plate with little cranium behind it; a dry fibre neck anchors it to a broad shallow torso; molded collar and pocket; a horizontal crop seam at the waist; broad gripping soles. Photographic skin tone appears on the face only; the rest is pale laminate and dark feltlike fibre.
- Behaviour: aligns, records the observer's position (a small chest aperture and a shutter sound mark the moment), points at the recorded position, commits to a straight lunge at that position, and recovers slowly with the opposed knees re-stacking. It does not steer after recording. It does not blink. Personnel are advised to move after the shutter sound.
- Notes: the office photographer's file contains a matching photograph, cropped at the waist. Subject's own paperwork is complete. The photograph matches the subject exactly. The subject matches the photograph exactly. File under PENDING.
- Origin: see attached photograph. The photograph is not an origin.
- Disposition: AWAITING ASSESSMENT. Indefinitely.

**Found text (photographer's note, portrait station, transit waiting room):**
> "Every standing portrait comes back missing its feet. The lens is fine; the floor is worn in grooves where they stand, and the image simply ends where the subject ends. Ask them to sit. The seated ones are worse: they come back with only a face, and the face is finished, calm, composed, as if it had practiced in the dark for years and was relieved to be seen."

## 11. The Erratum — rejected-stair organism

**Established: never human.** An aggregate-and-steel fungus that incorporated demolished stair material; its body and behaviour preserve a route a later renovation blocked. Whether that is memory, material organisation, or something like intention remains uncertain.

**NRRO INTAKE NR-160-E — STRUCTURAL DEFECT (ACTIVE)**
- Subject no.: NR-160-E. Received ██.██.19██. Class: DEFECT. Station: stratum 1, limestone ossuary, north cloister; most recently at the bricked doorway east of the library stair.
- Description: six jointed legs in three pairs about one elongated low trunk; eight overlapping tread vertebrae along the spine as articulated body parts; dark mortar cambium with steel fibres at the joints; short handrail feelers; low crushing head; a continuous soft ventral mortar band between the armoured flanks.
- Behaviour: stops, lowers the head, and traces a line with the feelers for about a second, then commits to a straight collision-tested ram, at most a second of charge. Impact staggers the subject roughly 1.2 seconds and exposes the ventral band. It cannot turn instantly. It does not ram at random: the line it measures is always the same old route.
- Notes: the modern sign calls the outlined doorway a wall. The worn threshold continues beneath the brickwork. The subject's tread widths match the removed stair sockets. File under Works, not under Persons.
- Origin: demolished stair material. Material is not an origin either.
- Disposition: DO NOT REMOVE THE INFILL.

**Found text (survey chalk, red, beside the bricked doorway):**
> "This stair was drawn. It was built. It is not on the plan. The plan is wrong, or the building is wrong, and one of them will have to be corrected. Every night something comes and measures the old route, head down, patient as a clerk. When the infill breaks, I will not report it. The stair has been in arrears for years and deserves its shortcut."

## 12. The Unaddressed — reader consortium

**Established: never human.** A consortium of species — glass that notices movement, cable that retains signal patterns, mortar that exerts force — discovering what counts as a reply. Coordination is incomplete; contact, recoil and injury may be misread as communication. No hidden human personality, no single unified mind.

**NRRO INTAKE NR-171-U — CORRESPONDENCE (UNANSWERED)**
- Subject no.: NR-171-U. Received ██.██.19██. Class: CORRESPONDENT. Station: stratum 4, root cathedral, the chamber before the still pool.
- Description: four splayed, articulated limbs beneath one broad low mantle; the front pair is longer and can lift to tap or sweep. Three overlapping glass louvres form a movable fan; behind it a dark signal knot and a rippled mortar pressure pad; amber translucent membranes and pale plaster baffles; no face.
- Behaviour: demonstrates a three-step sequence on the grooved floor plates (one, two and three grooves), then waits for a reply. A wrong reply brings a clearly prepared sweep of the front limbs along the threatened strip; the subject contracts first and shows where the strike will land. A correct reply calms the subject and it withdraws from the passage. It repeats the same sequence when asked, or when answered wrong, without ever changing the answer.
- Notes: the office has written to the subject eleven times. The letters were returned unopened, each with a single clean groove pressed into the envelope. Correspondence continues.
- Origin: the subject has been filing its replies where the office files its letters, and neither has ever read the other's.
- Disposition: DO NOT STOP WRITING.

**Found text (pencil, chair leg, root cathedral chamber):**
> "The chair replies when pressed. It is the only one that does. I have pressed it harder every day for a month, and it has never once repeated itself — each creak is new, which means it is listening, which means I am being answered. When the visitors pressed the wrong plate, I corrected them the only way I know. They keep pressing the right plate now. The chair is proud of me. I can tell by the creak."
