%%TITLE A Small Dictionary of Unclaimed Doors

#dept[Selected entries from an uncatalogued 1931 octavo found in the back room of a provincial bookseller, bound in faded ochre buckram without title page or colophon.]

#hw[Alabaster Threshold, The.] A low garden gate reported in three separate nineteenth-century memoirs as standing behind a walled orchard in southern England, a terraced vineyard near Prague, and a dry limestone ravine in the American Midwest. All three witnesses agree on the toll: whoever passes through returns with the exact memory of their ninth summer excised cleanly from the mind, leaving behind only the smell of wet cedar boards after a storm. A later hand has pencilled in the margin: _Not excised—merely shelved in a dry wing where the owner can no longer misplace it._

#hw[Chalk Hollow, The.] An unroofed limestone bowl where a reed-pipe is sometimes heard at civil twilight in late October. Unlike literary fairylands supervised by a sovereign guardian, the hollow keeps no chaperone. Visitors who remain past the third verse report an immediate distortion of bodily hunger and calendar time, accompanied by a fine, damp yellow dust packed beneath the fingernails that resists pumice soap for six days. _Cross-reference: See Pavilion of Twin Suns; see also the unclosed service of 30 November, where the dismissal was not said._

#hw[Door Forty-Four (Not an Exit).] A heavy painted steel door bearing a steady green transom plate, encountered at the end of long administrative corridors whenever a clerk has worked past the fourteenth hour of a shift. Though it resembles a fire exit, architectural drawings record no landing on the far side. Personnel are reminded that an emergency egress form—Form 44-B (_Room Undelivered_)—must be countersigned prior to approaching the latch. _Audit note: No copy of Form 44-B has ever been located in Stores._

#hw[Iron Pin At Section Sixteen, The.] A square-headed survey monument driven into bare dolomite on 13 October 1891 at the center of an unglaciated square mile where every neighboring section carries twenty feet of glacial gravel. All subsequent traverses around the four corner posts fail to close by 3.60 chains, always falling inward toward the pin. Compass needles refuse to settle within twenty-five paces of the southwest corner, and the rock on the north face stays warm in February. The chainman who signed the first four corner entries in the field book left the fifth line blank; his steel chain was found coiled neatly at the northwest post at dusk.

#hw[Pavilion Of Twin Suns, The.] A lakeside terrace entered not through a doorway, but through the second act of a banned two-act play that most bibliographers insist was never written—only quoted so densely across a century of footnotes and reviews that the accumulated hearsay precipitated a physical octavo. Unlike other portals, the Pavilion leaks outward: readers who recite its closing rubric at dusk rarely vanish into the lake; instead, their own rooms slowly acquire the acoustics of a stone amphitheater, and their mirrors cease to reflect the street behind them.

#hw[Room Forty-One (Fourth Floor).] A locked stores room in a three-storey limestone range whose staircase carries four landings between the ground and the first floor. Access is granted solely by heavy ward-pattern iron Key 118, delivered on a tarnished brass tray by an attendant in faded ochre livery whose start date on the staff list precedes the building's construction by eleven years. Those who ask why a three-storey building requires a fourth-floor key are politely informed that the stair has always served the upper floor, and that the drawings are simply awaiting assessment.

#hw[Waiting Chair (Entry 214).] A plain wooden chair placed at the far end of an intake gallery, reserved without interruption for an unnamed caller who presented at the desk on 2 June 1936 and was asked to wait. The appointment has been carried forward sixty-eight consecutive quarters onto Page 214 of the Appointments Ledger— despite a physical audit confirming that the ledger contains only 106 leaves (212 pages), leaving Pages 213 and 214 standing on no leaf at all.

%%LEXNOTE _I began this dictionary believing that if one catalogued enough doors, one might learn which of them led home. I have since checked the property book at the outer gate and found my own first name, the colour of my childhood bedroom, and what winter snow feels like entered on a single line under_ Equipment — Not Returnable. The seventh folder on my desk is tied with cotton tape and bears my handwriting on the cover. I am leaving it closed.
